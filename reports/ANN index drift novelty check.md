# The gap is real, but only at the join

**The combined proposal fills a real gap. Each of its four parts on its own has substantial precedent, so the novelty claim survives only if it is framed as a join of existing pieces.** No work found measures, on a real time-ordered IR test collection with relevance judgements for each snapshot, how the effectiveness gap between exact and approximate dense retrieval changes from snapshot to snapshot, or which index-maintenance actions recover that gap in nDCG terms. Part (a), the decomposition, is **real but narrower**. Exact-minus-ANN effectiveness has been measured on static collections since at least 2020, and the search-error/model-error split comes from machine translation. Only the longitudinal, relevance-labelled version is open. Part (b), rebuild versus incremental versus in-place maintenance, is **real but narrower**. Systems papers compare these at length in neighbour-recall units. PLAID SHIRTTT, AURORA, Embedding Surgery and MURR already measure some form of index maintenance or re-index avoidance by effectiveness, but none does so on real snapshots against an exact reference. Part (c), the distance-ratio drift signal, is **filled as a statistic**. Only its validation as a temporal predictor of index-induced loss remains open. Part (d), rebuild-versus-maintain guidance, is **real but narrow and derivative**. It stands only if (b) produces results, and a 2026 negative result on signal-triggered repair sets a hard bar. One premise in the current draft is wrong and must change before it goes to a DS@GT supervisor. LongEval's dense runs are exact by construction, so no published LongEval result contains index error that could have been misattributed. The real problem is that LongEval measures a system that is rarely deployed at scale. The search was adversarial but incomplete: structured database APIs and Google Scholar were blocked, and no forward-citation crawl was completed. The verdict therefore rests on targeted web search, full-text reads of the nine closest papers, and a full-text check of every 2025 and 2026 LongEval working-notes paper.

## Every part has precedent; only the combination is unoccupied

The table gives the verdict for each component and the narrow slice that remains open. "Filled" means a reviewer could cite prior work that already does the thing as stated in the draft.

| Part | Verdict | What already exists | What remains open |
|---|---|---|---|
| (a) Exact vs index decomposition per snapshot | **Real but narrower** | Static exact-minus-HNSW nDCG (Lin 2024; Tu et al. 2020; Macdonald and Tonellotto 2021; Leto et al. 2024); search vs model error via exact search (Stahlberg and Byrne 2019); index degradation over time in recall units (DeDrift, Quake); early-fit centroids collapsing nDCG on a date-ordered stream, measured against an approximate oracle (PLAID SHIRTTT) | Tracking the exact-minus-ANN term in relevance units across real snapshots, each with its own qrels |
| (b) Rebuild vs incremental vs in-place, by effectiveness | **Real but narrower** | Recall-based comparisons (FreshDiskANN, SPFresh, IP-DiskANN, Quake, Ada-IVF, DeDrift, When to Repair, Cosmos DB DiskANN); effectiveness-based but non-longitudinal or synthetic (AURORA, Embedding Surgery, MURR, PLAID SHIRTTT) | HNSW/IVF/SQ maintenance on real LongEval snapshots, judged by nDCG against both exact search and a full rebuild |
| (c) Label-free query-to-NN / row-to-NN ratio | **Filled as a statistic; real but narrow as a validated temporal alarm** | NN-distance OOD scores (Sun et al. 2022); OOD distance ratios (RoarGraph, OOD-DiskANN); relative contrast (VIBE, Steiner-hardness); label-free recall estimators (DARTH, Quake APS); label-free refresh triggers (GLHF at LongEval 2026, GradNormIR) | Whether the ratio, controlled for corpus size, predicts the measured index-induced loss on new queries better than these baselines |
| (d) Rebuild-vs-maintain guidance | **Real but narrow; derivative of (b)** | Count-based production triggers (Milvus, Qdrant, OceanBase); Quake's cost model; When to Repair's negative result that triggered repair does not beat fixed cadence | A decision rule stated in relevance-effectiveness terms and evaluated against an oracle on real IR data |
| Combination (a)+(b)+(c) on LongEval | **Real** | Nothing found | The whole study |

The **decomposition** is the weakest claim to originality and the strongest piece of method. Jimmy Lin's controlled Lucene study already measures what the proposal calls the index term on static BEIR collections. It finds HNSW negligible below 100K documents, under 0.01 nDCG@10 between 100K and 1M, and about 0.015 below flat search on BioASQ's 15M documents, and it warns that HNSW build non-determinism matters when comparing models of similar quality ([Lin, arXiv 2409.06464](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.06464v1)). Tu et al. had reported ANN-versus-exact effectiveness gaps for dense retrieval in 2020 ([Tu et al., ICTIR 2020](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~jimmylin/publications/Tu_etal_ICTIR2020.pdf)). Leto et al. held the encoder fixed and varied ANN accuracy, finding that a search recall of 0.7 costs only 2–3% gold-document recall ([Leto et al., arXiv 2411.07396](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.07396v1)). The conceptual move, using exact search as an oracle to separate search error from model error, is Stahlberg and Byrne's for NMT, where search errors were shown to mask model errors ([Stahlberg and Byrne, EMNLP 2019](https://aclanthology.org/D19-1331/)). The proposal should claim that lineage, not the idea. What no one has done is follow this quantity over time on a test collection whose qrels change with each snapshot. Keller, Breuer and Schaer decompose temporal change by test-collection component (documents, topics, qrels) and hold the system fixed, with no index term ([Keller et al., ICTIR 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01373)). The proposal adds a system-internal component to that environment-side decomposition. That is a clean and citable extension. Arithmetically, it is an accounting identity. The index term interacts with corpus size and query distribution, so it should be called a component of the observed change, not a causal share.

The **maintenance comparison** is crowded in recall units and thin in relevance units. Quake replays real monthly Wikipedia additions from April 2013 to December 2021, 103 updates growing from 1.6M to 12M vectors. It shows that unmaintained Faiss-IVF degrades while its own maintenance keeps recall stable, and it compares DeDrift, LIRE and HNSW. But its queries are page embeddings sampled by page views, and its metric is recall against exact neighbours ([Quake, arXiv 2506.03437](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.03437)). The four effectiveness-measured precedents each miss at least two of the proposal's ingredients. PLAID SHIRTTT is the one that hits closest. It reports that centroids fit on the first 5% of chronologically ordered NeuCLIR Chinese documents drop nDCG@20 from 0.440 to 0.047, so index staleness on a date-ordered stream is already shown to destroy relevance effectiveness ([PLAID SHIRTTT, arXiv 2405.00975](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.00975)). Its reference is an oracle PLAID index, which is still approximate and compressed, so centroid staleness and ordinary approximation error stay entangled. It uses one fixed qrel set, not evolving snapshots.

The **drift signal** cannot be claimed as new. The k-th nearest-neighbour distance is a standard label-free OOD score ([Sun et al., ICML 2022](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.06507v3)). RoarGraph characterises OOD queries by their distance to nearest neighbours relative to base-to-base distances, which is essentially the proposal's ratio, and reports OOD queries 2.1x to 11.3x farther from their neighbours ([RoarGraph, arXiv 2408.08933](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.08933v1)). DARTH's per-query recall predictor already relies on query-side NN-distance features ([DARTH, arXiv 2505.19001](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19001v1)). Practitioner blogs recommend week-to-week "nearest-neighbour stability" monitoring ([tianpan.co](https://tianpan.co/blog/2026-04-19-embedding-drift-long-lived-rag-systems)). Inside LongEval itself, team GLHF proposed label-free weekly refresh triggers built on ingestion velocity, staleness rate and coverage gap ([Yang and Wu, arXiv 2607.04088](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.04088)). What remains is an empirical test: does a size-controlled version of the ratio predict index-induced loss before it happens, and does it beat these baselines?

The **guidance** inherits a hard null. The 2026 "When to Repair" paper ran Vamana under synthetic bursty churn. At a matched repair budget, signal-triggered repair "does not beat fixed-cadence repair on tail recall—on either dataset, at any of three graph degrees, or at a 5× larger index". Its leading-indicator test failed in 14 of 16 cells, and the authors withdrew their v1 claim as an "interpolated baseline trap" ([Mandarapu and Kunkunuru, arXiv 2607.00728](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.00728)). Any rule the proposal recommends has to beat a fixed cadence and a count trigger at matched cost. The honest prior is that it will not, so "rebuild on a schedule, recalibrate effort every snapshot" must be an acceptable outcome.

## LongEval's dense runs are exact, so nothing has been misattributed yet

The draft says evaluations "treat this approximation as exact" and that index error is currently booked as model or data drift. For LongEval this is factually wrong. A full-text search of all twelve 2026 LongEval working-notes papers found **no ANN first-stage retrieval, no index configuration and no exact-versus-approximate comparison**. Only 3 of the 20 Task 1 runs used a dense neural component ([Extended Overview of CLEF 2026 LongEval, CEUR Vol-4283 paper 374](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4283/paper374.pdf)). The organisers' Qwen3-Embedding-4B baseline uses pyterrier-dr's `NumpyRetriever`, which is exhaustive exact search, rebuilt for each snapshot ([baseline ir-metadata.yml](https://github.com/clef-longeval/longeval-code/blob/main/clef26/scientific-retrieval/baseline-pyterrier-dense/baseline-qwen3-embedding-4b/ir-metadata.yml); [pyterrier-dr](https://github.com/terrierteam/pyterrier_dr)). Earlier years used a Faiss flat index ([Keller et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.10549)). The 2026 papers come close to the proposal's question only in passing. Team Lexora reused the pre-built per-snapshot exact indexes and listed "online dense index updates" as something it could not assess ([Kumaran D et al., paper 379](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4283/paper379.pdf)). Team GLHF treated refresh as a scheduling layer and explicitly disclaimed any causal claim about effectiveness ([Wu and Yang, paper 384](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4283/paper384.pdf)). One note in this round attributed an "HNSW index built over the same corpus" snippet to paper 384 and labelled 384 as the Candidate-Constrained RAG paper. The full-text check shows that paper is 385 and that it uses no vector index ([paper 385](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4283/paper385.pdf)), so that snippet should be disregarded.

The same exact-search assumption holds next door. "Still Fresh?" compares two real FreshStack snapshots (October 2024 and October 2025) across about 15 dense embedders with LLM-judged nuggets. It attributes the score changes to the corpus and relevance shift and says nothing about exact versus approximate search ([Kuissi et al., arXiv 2603.04532](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04532)). Kim et al.'s study of dynamic corpora states outright that "for DE, we employ the exhaustive (brute-force) search method" ([Kim et al., EMNLP 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.755.pdf)). So the field's longitudinal findings describe idealised exact systems. That is the stronger and true version of the premise: **nobody knows whether LongEval's temporal-robustness conclusions transfer to systems that search approximately**, and operators watching their own drift cannot tell index error from model drift. The scale argument helps here. LongEval 2025 Web had about 14M test documents ([LongEval 2025 overview](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.17469v1)), which is the range where Lin measured 0.011–0.020 nDCG@10 HNSW losses.

Several further works sit one step further away, and the proposal should cite them so a reviewer cannot. On the systems side, these are graph and partition maintenance measured by recall: [FreshDiskANN](https://arxiv.org/abs/2105.09613), [SPFresh](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.14452), [IP-DiskANN](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.13826), [Ada-IVF](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00970), [Xiao et al.'s unreachable-points study](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.07871), the [CANDY benchmark](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.19651), [streaming quantization](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.18335) and [Azure Cosmos DB DiskANN streaming recall](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/cosmosdb/azure-cosmos-db-with-diskann-part-4-stable-vector-search-recall-with-streaming-data/). On the IR side, these avoid re-indexing by changing the model, all of them different from the proposal: [Query Drift Compensation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.00037), [L²R](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.11512), [CLEVER](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.14968) and [Drift-Adapter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23471). On the operational side, live recall monitoring by sampling exact search is established: turbopuffer samples 1% of production queries ([turbopuffer](https://turbopuffer.com/blog/continuous-recall)), and Pinterest computes ANN and KNN side by side in serving ([Pinterest Engineering](https://medium.com/pinterest-engineering/evolving-pinterests-embedding-retrieval-platform-aede4e831e01)). **"Monitoring index recall over time" is therefore not novel either.** Only the relevance-unit attribution on a real longitudinal collection is.

The pattern across all of this is consistent. Two literatures each cover one half of the proposal and never meet. Systems papers track index error over time but only as neighbour recall on synthetic runbooks or qrel-free workloads. IR papers track effectiveness over time but with exact search or with the index held fixed. The HLTCOE group behind both PLAID SHIRTTT and MURR is the one most likely to close the gap first, because it already has streaming dense indexes and relevance-labelled date-ordered collections.

## About a hundred queries ran; the citation graph was not crawled

The coverage is broad but not systematic, and the novelty claim is only as strong as this caveat allows. The systematic sweep sent 81 structured queries. **21 of 25 Semantic Scholar queries hit HTTP 429 rate limits, all 14 arXiv API queries were rate-limited, and all 30 DBLP queries hit a bot check**, so only about 16 of those ran. Several "nothing found" results from that pass mean "not executed", not "executed with no hits". The other passes added roughly 70 general web searches and about 60 page fetches across four angles: search-vs-model error, evolving-corpus retrieval, industry index staleness, and a 27-query adversarial re-sweep. Full texts were read for AURORA, PLAID SHIRTTT, Quake (the first 100K of 114K characters), When to Repair, Still Fresh?, Streaming RAG, ReproRAG and the JISEM ETL review, plus every 2025 and 2026 LongEval working-notes paper. Some arXiv HTML reads went through a summarising fetch tool, and the quotes from those were not checked line by line against the PDFs. One earlier fetch summary hallucinated FAISS and HNSW in the 2024 LongEval overview, and full-text search disproved it. So every "LongEval never did X" claim here rests on direct text search.

Specific blocks matter for the verdict. Google Scholar "cited by" pages redirected and were treated as blocked, and the Semantic Scholar page for PLAID SHIRTTT returned 404. **No forward-citation list was obtained for DeDrift, PLAID SHIRTTT, Keller ICTIR 2024, Lin 2024, FreshDiskANN, SPFresh, Quake or MURR.** A paper that cites one of these and reports nDCG over time under ANN maintenance is the most likely way the gap is already filled, and this search could not rule it out. BANCO's full text returned HTTP 403 from the ACM DL. Pinterest's engineering posts returned 403. Embedding Surgery, GradNormIR, L²R and MURR's reference list were seen through abstracts or snippets rather than read in full. Not checked at all: the ReNeuIR 2023, 2024 and 2026 paper lists; SIGIR 2025 and 2026 short papers; Big-ANN NeurIPS 2023 streaming-track metrics; thesis repositories (ProQuest, EThOS, OATD); vendor documentation for Elastic, OpenSearch, Milvus, Weaviate and Pinecone; and "How important is Recall for Measuring Retrieval Quality?" ([arXiv 2512.20854](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.20854)), which appeared in results but was not read. One source in the notes credited DeDrift with "95–99% recall at 100x lower cost". Those figures match Drift-Adapter's abstract, not DeDrift's, so they are excluded here.

## A three-snapshot pilot and three citation pages decide it

Two kinds of evidence would settle the question faster than more keyword searching. The first is **a pilot that measures the index term directly**. The organisers publish per-snapshot exact Qwen3-Embedding-4B indexes for LongEval 2026 Sci on HuggingFace, and pyterrier-dr offers a FAISS HNSW retriever as a drop-in approximate counterpart ([baseline README](https://github.com/clef-longeval/longeval-code/tree/main/clef26/scientific-retrieval/baseline-pyterrier-dense); [pyterrier-dr](https://github.com/terrierteam/pyterrier_dr)). Reusing those embeddings avoids the roughly one-V100-day re-embedding cost DS@GT reported per split ([DS@GT at LongEval 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.08360)). The pilot needs four things:

- **The index term per snapshot.** Compute I_t = nDCG_exact − nDCG_ANN on each of the three snapshots, for a per-snapshot rebuild and for a time-ordered incremental build.
- **Fixed operating points.** Set search effort so that recall against exact search is about 0.90, 0.95 and 0.99.
- **A size-matched control.** Rebuild on a random subsample of a later snapshot equal in size to snapshot 1, so that the effect of growth can be separated from the effect of content drift.
- **Noise estimates.** Use three to five build seeds and paired permutation tests per query, and report judged@10, because unjudged near-tie documents bias the measured effect toward zero.

Then read the result. The "index error grows over time" hypothesis is dead if the change in I_t across snapshots stays within seed-to-seed spread at every operating point. Lin's five BioASQ builds varied by up to 0.009 nDCG@10, which gives a rough noise floor ([Lin](https://arxiv.org/html/2409.06464v1)). The novelty survives a null, but the paper changes from "hidden variable" to "measured licence to ignore the index above recall X". A growing I_t that survives the size control is the strongest possible support. Two data limits are unverified: the 2026 per-snapshot query counts, and whether CORE ever removes documents. If CORE is append-only, the graph-repair arm has little to repair, and the 2025 Web task becomes the better second dataset. It has monthly snapshots, document removals and 63,416 test queries ([LongEval 2025 overview](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.17469v1)).

The second kind of evidence is **hand-checking the citation graph in a normal browser**. In priority order: Google Scholar "cited by" for [DeDrift](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.02752), [PLAID SHIRTTT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00975) and [Keller et al. ICTIR 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01373), then [Lin 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.06464), [Quake](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.03437) and [MURR](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.10250). Filter for nDCG or MAP together with "stream", "temporal", "snapshot" or "update". Also download BANCO's PDF from the ACM DL to confirm its datasets and metrics, read Embedding Surgery's full text for any temporal experiment, and scan recent work by the HLTCOE authors (Lawrie, Yang, Mayfield, Oard) for a SHIRTTT follow-up against exact search. Any single hit reporting relevance effectiveness under ANN maintenance on time-ordered snapshots with an exact baseline would collapse the combination claim to "replication on LongEval". Two lighter hits would narrow it further. One is a 2027 LongEval call that adds an efficiency or index track. The other is BANCO turning out to use labelled IR collections.

## Wording to change before the supervisor sees it

The draft's argument holds, but six sentences invite easy rebuttal. The table gives replacements.

| Current draft wording | Problem | Recommended wording |
|---|---|---|
| "Evaluations usually treat this approximation as exact" | LongEval's dense runs *are* exact (NumPy brute force, rebuilt per snapshot) | "LongEval evaluates dense systems with exact search, so its drift findings describe a system rarely deployed at scale; what approximate search adds over time is unmeasured" |
| "Each of these adds error that a longitudinal evaluation would currently book as 'the model got worse'" | No LongEval run contains ANN error to misattribute | "In deployed systems, where search is approximate and recall is rarely monitored against relevance, index error is indistinguishable from model or data drift" |
| "There is little evidence on when it is actually needed" | FreshDiskANN, SPFresh, Quake, DeDrift, When to Repair; 20% production thresholds | "Rebuild versus maintenance is well studied in neighbour-recall terms on synthetic or qrel-free workloads, but not on real evolving IR collections with relevance judgements" |
| "A way to separate the index's share of quality drift from the rest" (presented as the contribution) | Static exact-minus-ANN is established (Lin; Tu et al.; Leto et al.) and the split is Stahlberg and Byrne's | "We extend Keller et al.'s component decomposition of temporal change with a system-internal index term, exact minus approximate, following the search-error/model-error method of Stahlberg and Byrne; the identity is accounting, and the index term interacts with corpus size" |
| "A judgement-free signal for query drift" (presented as new) | The NN-distance ratio is a known OOD statistic (Sun et al.; RoarGraph; DARTH) | "We test whether a known label-free NN-distance statistic, controlled for corpus growth, predicts index-induced loss on new queries better than standard OOD and hardness baselines" |
| "My expectation is that ... targeted maintenance recovers most of it at a fraction of a rebuild's cost" | This is the systems literature's finding in recall units | "Systems work finds that local maintenance recovers most recall cheaply; we test whether that holds for relevance effectiveness, and whether any drift trigger beats a fixed cadence at matched budget" |

Three further edits strengthen the pitch. First, add a related-work paragraph that names PLAID SHIRTTT, MURR, Embedding Surgery, AURORA, Quake, DeDrift and When to Repair, and states the separating line for each. A supervisor who knows SHIRTTT and sees it missing will discount the rest. Second, open the "Fit with LongEval" section with DS@GT's own 2025 suggestion to analyse "the semantic k-NN graph of a dense embedding model … in the context of the temporal evolution of systems" ([DS@GT at LongEval 2025](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2507.08360)), since the proposal follows directly from it. Third, change "recall against exact search ... isolates the index's effect cleanly" to report recall, a distance-ratio measure and nDCG together. Recall against exact search over-predicts downstream loss because most ANN misses are near-ties ([Dimitropoulos and Mamoulis, arXiv 2606.04522](https://arxiv.org/html/2606.04522)).

## Conclusion

The author's skepticism is half right. Each piece of the proposal has been done somewhere: the decomposition statically, the maintenance comparison in recall units, the drift statistic for OOD queries, and the guidance as production heuristics and one careful negative result. A reviewer who sees the parts separately will call it incremental. What has not been done is the join, and the join answers a question neither community can answer alone: do conclusions about temporal robustness, drawn with exact search, survive the approximate search that deployed systems actually run? The narrowest defensible claim is this: *the first measurement, on a real time-ordered IR test collection with relevance judgements for each snapshot, of how the exact-minus-approximate effectiveness gap changes across snapshots, and of how much of it a rebuild, incremental insertion and specific in-place maintenance actions each recover, reported in nDCG alongside recall against exact search.*

That claim is modest, falsifiable and cheap to test. The released exact indexes make the pilot a matter of days, not months, and the pilot decides more than any further literature search could. A flat index term at every operating point turns the project into a short, useful "licence to ignore" note for the lab. A term that grows after the size control gives a paper neither IR nor systems research has written. Either way, the pitch should lead with the claim above and with the three citation checks already done. The research statement's own reference list currently omits every one of the close prior works, and that gap is the first thing to fix.
