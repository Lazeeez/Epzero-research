# Prior work on query-driven graph repair and on ANN parameter auto-tuning or re-calibration under collection growth

Scope: claim (6), that four query-driven graph repairs raise recall at a fixed setting but none beats a wider search at equal distance work, and claim (4b), that a 50-query calibration over the ef grid {100,200,400,800} at a 95% recall target can miss a one-point recall drift as the collection grows. Note: arXiv HTML/abstract pages were read with a summarising fetch tool. The text marked as quotes is what that tool returned, and short excerpts are verbatim per the tool. PDFs (NGFix PDF, DARTH PDF) could not be text-extracted, so I relied on the ar5iv/arXiv HTML versions.

## (a) Query-driven repair or augmentation of graph ANN indexes: how gains are reported, and whether any compare against wider search at matched work

### Takeaway
The most dangerous prior work is NGFix/RFix (SIGMOD 2026). Its authors report QPS–recall and NDC–rderr curves on which their repaired HNSW beats plain HNSW across the ef sweep. That is a matched-cost Pareto comparison, so it implicitly compares against "plain HNSW searched wider", and it claims a win. This directly contradicts claim (6) for NGFix, unless the regime differs: NGFix uses roughly 1M–10M historical queries with exact kNN on cross-modal OOD data, while the paper uses a small calibration set. I found no paper that reports the four repairs at a fixed ef and then, as a separate explicit test, against a wider ef at equal NDC. The nearest methodological precedent is a 2026 arXiv "matched-budget negative result" on graph repair. It matches repair passes, not query distance work.

### Cited Findings
- **NGFix/RFix**: Hua, Mo, et al. (Nankai University / Alibaba), "Dynamically Detect and Fix Hardness for Efficient Approximate Nearest Neighbor Search", PACMMOD 3(6), Dec 2025 (SIGMOD 2026), DOI 10.1145/3769783, arXiv 2510.22316 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.22316); [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2510.22316)
  - Metrics: "We use query-per-second (QPS) and Number of Distance Calculations (NDC) to measure efficiency." Figure 8 "presents the QPS–recall@100 and NDC–rderr@100 curves for various graph indexes across four cross-modal datasets." NDC is plotted against rderr (relative distance error), not against recall — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2510.22316)
  - Against HNSW, the comparison is at matched QPS ("1.78–6.88 times higher than HNSW"). Against RoarGraph it is at matched NDC: "When rderr@100 is less than 0.0001, HNSW-NGFix* requires only 48.5%–78% of the distance calculations needed by RoarGraph." — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2510.22316)
  - The authors explicitly reject wider search as the remedy: "A common and simple approach to address this is to increase the search list size, but this will lead to an increase in search latency"; "A more effective solution is to design an improved graph index that guides the greedy search more effectively" — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2510.22316)
  - Query volume: about 10M historical queries for Text-to-Image10M, LAION10M, SIFT10M and DEEP10M, 2.5M for WebVid2.5M, and 1M for MainSearch. "We use brute-force to calculate the exact kNN of each historical query." Approximate kNN is offered as an alternative with only a slight QPS drop. Test queries are "entirely different from historical queries." — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2510.22316)
  - Overhead: extra out-degree is capped at M_EX = 48, each extra edge stores a 16-bit Escape-Hardness value, and the paper bounds added edges at 2(Nq−1) per query — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2510.22316)
  - Workload drift: "we periodically delete a subset of existing extra edges added by NGFix* to make room for newly added edges" — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.arxiv.org/html/2510.22316)
  - Build speed: index construction is 2.35–9.02× faster than RoarGraph — [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.22316)
- **RoarGraph**: Chen et al., "RoarGraph: A Projected Bipartite Graph for Efficient Cross-Modal Approximate Nearest Neighbor Search", PVLDB 17(11):2735–2749, VLDB 2024. It builds a query–base bipartite graph from historical queries and projects it onto the base vectors. It claims "up to 3.56× faster search speed at a 90% recall rate for OOD queries", a matched-recall QPS comparison along an L sweep — [PVLDB PDF](https://vldb.org/pvldb/vol17/p2735-chen.pdf); [arXiv 2408.08933](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08933)
- **OOD-DiskANN**: Jaiswal, Krishnaswamy, Garg, Simhadri, Agrawal, arXiv 2211.12850 (2022). Search snippets say it was later published at WWW 2023; I did not verify the venue. It uses "a sparing sample (1% of index set size) of OOD queries" in construction. It claims "up to 40% improvement in mean query latency over SoTA algorithms of a similar memory footprint." It diagnoses that the nearest neighbours of OOD queries are "much less connected" in Vamana graphs. It also reports that for a fixed recall target, OOD-query latency is worse by an order of magnitude or more — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.12850)
- **EnhanceGraph**: Zhong, Jin, Cheng, et al. (Tongji / Ant Group), arXiv 2506.13144 (June 2025, preprint). It adds a "conjugate graph" with edges from greedy-search local optima to true nearest neighbours, taken from search logs, self-generated interpolated queries and pruned construction edges. That is the same idea as witness edges built from logged queries. It reports Recall@k and QPS only, with no NDC. It reports no equal-L or equal-NDC control. Instead it makes an analytic claim that the overhead is "equivalent to increasing the search parameter L by 2". GIST1M Recall@1 goes from 41.74% to 93.42% (τ-MNG), with NSG QPS going from 267.3 to 260.0 — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.13144v2)
- **Steiner-hardness**: Wang et al., PVLDB 17(13):4668–4682 (2024). It is a connection-based measure of query hardness. Its correlation with real query effort is 0.75, against 0.42 for LID. Effort is measured as the NDC needed to reach a recall level. It notes that standard workloads "are dominated by easy queries, leading to over-optimistic results" — [PVLDB PDF](https://vldb.org/pvldb/vol17/p4668-wang.pdf); [arXiv 2408.13899](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.13899v1)
- **Closest methodological precedent for a matched-budget negative result**: Mandarapu & Kunkunuru, "When to Repair a Graph ANN Index: A Matched-Budget Negative Result, and the Interpolated-Baseline Trap That Hid It", arXiv 2607.00728 v2 (10 July 2026). It asks whether repair triggered by a navigability signal beats fixed-cadence repair: "At matched repair budget, it does not." "recall is sharply concave in repair budget -- one consolidation captures over half of all achievable gain". The v1 result was positive only because the baseline was "read off a straight line between zero and four passes". The budget unit is consolidation passes, not query NDC, and the paper does not compare against wider ef — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.00728). The v1 title was "Navigability-Signal-Triggered Local Repair Protects Tail Recall Under Bursty Churn" — [chatpaper mirror](https://chatpaper.com/es/paper/306326)
- **Adjacent workshop paper**: "Coverage Is Not Redundancy: Maintenance Cost and Exposure of Query-Aware Admission Indexes in Vector Databases Under Workload Drift" (Pathak, arXiv 2608.16043, Aug 2026). It treats a query-aware sentinel set as an auxiliary index that must be maintained under drift and uses a "recall-aware witness probe". The word "witness" collides with the paper's "witness edges", but the mechanism is different (admission gating, not graph edges). It has no comparison against wider search — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16043)
- **Adaptive early termination** (per-query, so effectively a "search wider only where needed" baseline):
  - DARTH: PACMMOD 3(4) (SIGMOD), DOI 10.1145/3749160, arXiv 2505.19001. It is "trained on 10K queries randomly sampled from the learning sets". Its speedup is measured against "the plain search of the index without early termination". For the REM baseline, "we need to explicitly tune the HNSW parameters for each recall target", and REM "still relies on fixed parameters for the entire query workload and cannot adjust to the hardness of individual queries." — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.19001v2)
  - LAET (Li et al., SIGMOD 2020, "Improving approximate nearest neighbor search through learned adaptive early termination") uses Gradient Boosting Decision Trees to predict a per-query stopping condition. This description is as summarised in [Ada-ef §2](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.06636v1); I did not fetch the primary paper.

### Inferences
- A reviewer saying "already known" would most likely say that matched-cost Pareto curves (QPS–recall, NDC–recall) are the standard ANN evaluation, and that NGFix and RoarGraph already report them and win. So the paper's novelty cannot be "comparing at equal work". It has to be the negative finding in a specific regime: a small calibration-query set, in-distribution or near-distribution text-retrieval queries, and the LongEval collection. The paper must explain why its NGFix result differs from the published one. Plausible reasons are many fewer repair queries than NGFix's 1M–10M, ID rather than cross-modal OOD queries, and recall@k rather than rderr. These are inferences and need checking against the paper's setup.
- NGFix's matched-NDC comparison is against RoarGraph and is plotted against rderr. Its HNSW comparison is at matched QPS. A matched-NDC, matched-recall comparison against plain HNSW with larger ef is therefore not explicitly reported as a headline. That is a defensible gap the paper can occupy.
- EnhanceGraph reports Recall@1 gains at near-equal QPS but never runs a "same graph, L+2 or larger L" control. Its reported gains on GIST1M and τ-MNG are large, so wider search alone may not close them. A reviewer could cite this as counter-evidence to "repairs never beat wider search", at least for recall@1 with low-degree graphs.
- Frontier resumption from skipped candidates is conceptually a form of wider or continued search. Whether it beats wider search at equal NDC is close to the question adaptive early termination already studies (LAET, DARTH, Ada-ef): those methods spend extra search only on hard queries. I found no prior "resume from pruned frontier" repair paper under that name, which is unverified.

### Gaps
- I found no paper that evaluates encoder-generated pseudo-query bridging edges. EnhanceGraph's interpolated self-generated queries are the closest, but they are geometric interpolations, not encoder-generated text queries.
- I could not extract the NGFix PDF tables, so I have no exact NDC-at-recall numbers against HNSW. The last ~30k characters of the ar5iv page were not read; these may include the appendix or ablations.
- I did not check the OOD-DiskANN full text for whether it uses NDC or latency at matched recall.
- I did not check the code repositories (matchyc/RoarGraph, the NGFix code) for their exact evaluation protocols.

## (b) Automatic tuning or re-calibration of ef/nprobe as the collection or workload changes

### Takeaway
Two kinds of prior work exist. Static auto-tuners (Sun et al. ICLR 2023, VDTuner ICDE 2024) tune once against a recall or cost target. Per-query adaptive methods (Ada-ef, DARTH, LAET, Quake's APS) set the search budget at query time. Quake states outright that a static nprobe loses recall as the index changes ("LIRE's recall degrades over time as it uses a static nprobe"). Ada-ef measures a stale calibration falling just below the 0.95 target after 50% insertions (0.945 average recall). That is close to the paper's claim (4b), and a reviewer could call the qualitative point known. I found no paper studying a coarse doubling ef grid calibrated on about 50 queries and its power to detect a one-point drift.

### Cited Findings
- **Sun, Guo, Kumar**, "Automating Nearest Neighbor Search Configuration with Constrained Optimization", ICLR 2023 (not 2024). It tunes quantisation-based ANN (ScaNN): the method "takes just a desired search cost or recall as input" and produces tunings near the speed–recall Pareto frontier. It is a one-shot tuning; I found no re-tuning under growth in the abstract — [arXiv 2301.01702](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.01702); [DBLP](https://dblp.uni-trier.de/rec/conf/iclr/SunGK23.html)
- **VDTuner** (Yang et al., ICDE 2024): multi-objective Bayesian optimisation over index type, index parameters and system parameters on Milvus. It reports up to 14.12% better search speed and 186.38% better recall than default settings. It is a one-shot tuner — [arXiv 2404.10413](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.10413); [code](https://github.com/tiannuo-yang/VDTuner)
- **Ada-ef**: Zhang & Miller (Waterloo), "Distribution-Aware Exploration for Adaptive HNSW Search", arXiv 2512.06636 (Dec 2025). An aggregator gives the venue as PACMMOD, DOI 10.1145/3786639; I did not verify this — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.06636v1)
  - It sets "an ef value for each individual query vector to approximately achieve a target recall".
  - Its calibration uses data vectors as pseudo-queries, not real queries: "sampling 200 data vectors and computing their ground truth".
  - On the static setting: "Once configured, the ef value remains fixed and is applied uniformly across the entire query workload"; "However, this is not guaranteed" (meaning consistently high recall under a fixed ef).
  - On updates (§7.5): "the ef-estimation table can be recomputed with respect to the updated HNSW index". Stale Ada-ef "may fall slightly below the target recall, particularly with large batch sizes (e.g., 50% insertion on DeepImage)". Table 5 shows stale average recall of 0.945 against the 0.95 target, with P5 at 0.820 and P1 at 0.720. Under deletion, stale recall stays above target (0.954–0.979). The datasets are DeepImage and MS MARCO V2.1, with batch sizes of 10% and 50%. This is the closest precedent to claim (4b): a drift of about half a point in mean recall, while the tails are far worse.
- **Quake**: "Quake: Adaptive Indexing for Vector Search", arXiv 2506.03437 v2 (June 2025). I believe it appeared at OSDI 2025, but the arXiv page does not state a venue, so this is unverified — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.03437v2)
  - "we find these methods degrade recall as nprobe needs to change as the index structure changes."
  - A static nprobe "does not adapt to changing data distributions or query patterns, leading to either insufficient recall or excessive data scanning." (§2.3)
  - "LIRE's recall degrades over time as it uses a static nprobe." (§7.3; LIRE is SPFresh's rebalancing protocol)
  - APS "requires no offline tuning" and gets "only a 17–29% increase in latency relative to the oracle" on Sift1M. Auncel's "volume estimation requires calibration."
- **DeDrift**: Baranchuk, Douze, Upadhyay, Yalniz, ICCV 2023. "Unless a costly index reconstruction is performed frequently, content drift degrades the search accuracy and efficiency"; DeDrift "almost eliminates the accuracy degradation" and is up to 100× faster than a full rebuild. It adapts IVF/PQ quantisers rather than search parameters — [CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content/ICCV2023/html/Baranchuk_DEDRIFT_Robust_Similarity_Search_under_Content_Drift_ICCV_2023_paper.html); [arXiv 2308.02752](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.02752)
- **DARTH**: trained once per dataset, it can serve any recall target, but the HTML I read does not address inserts or retraining. It notes that competing tuning approaches "have to be tuned from the start if the recall target changes." — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.19001v2)
- **Industry**: an Azure Cosmos DB DiskANN post (May 2025) reports that recall "can degrade significantly if updates and deletes aren't handled carefully". This comes from a search snippet and was not fetched; treat it as unverified — [Microsoft devblog](https://devblogs.microsoft.com/cosmosdb/?p=10095)

### Inferences
- "Fixed search parameters lose recall as the collection changes" is already stated by Quake and measured by Ada-ef (stale table, 50% insertions), so a reviewer could say it is known. The paper's claim (4b) is narrower and still appears new: whether a small, coarse calibration (50 queries, doubling grid, 95% mean target) has the statistical resolution to detect a one-point drift. Its framing, that a calibrated setting protects only if calibration detects the drift, is a power-analysis point I did not find stated anywhere.
- Ada-ef's 0.945 average next to 0.72 P1 recall supports a related argument: a mean-recall calibration target hides tail damage.
- Calibration sizes vary widely across prior work: 200 sampled data vectors (Ada-ef), 10K learning-set queries (DARTH), about 1% of the index (OOD-DiskANN), and 1M–10M historical queries (NGFix, RoarGraph). Fifty queries is far smaller than any of these, which strengthens the "detection power" angle but invites a "use more calibration queries" objection.
- Per-query adaptive methods (APS, Ada-ef, DARTH) are the natural rebuttal: "why calibrate a global ef at all?" The paper should position against them.

### Gaps
- I did not fetch the primary texts of FreshDiskANN (2021), SPFresh/LIRE (SOSP 2023), IP-DiskANN (2025) or Ada-IVF (2024). I cannot confirm whether they plot recall at fixed parameters over time or recommend re-calibration. Quake's statement about LIRE is the only second-hand evidence.
- I could not confirm Quake's OSDI 2025 venue or Ada-ef's PACMMOD venue from primary pages.
- I found no study of ef-grid granularity, or of the number of calibration queries needed to detect a recall drift of a given size.
- I did not fetch the full LAET (SIGMOD 2020) paper, so its training-query count and update handling are unverified.
