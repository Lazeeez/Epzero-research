# Pooling bias, unjudged documents, and LLM relevance judgments (novelty review for claim 5)

Claim under review: "Most documents an ANN index substitutes into the top 10 are unjudged when judgments were pooled from exact-style runs; condensed-list nDCG (unjudged removed) shrinks the measured ANN-minus-exact gap by roughly half to three-quarters, i.e. part of the ANN 'loss' is a pooling artifact." Plus validity of LLM relevance labels (LongEval GPT-OSS-120B primary; GPT-OSS-20B, click raw/DCTR, 29 human-judged queries as sensitivity).

Verification legend: **[V]** = text or numbers checked against the primary source in this session. **[S]** = confirmed only through search-engine summaries or abstract pages, not full text. **[U]** = cited from prior knowledge and NOT checked this session; check before citing.

## Q1. Classic pooling bias and unjudged-document work (Zobel, Buckley & Voorhees, Sakai, Yilmaz & Aslam, Webber, Lipani, Voorhees)

### Takeaway
None of this is new in general. Since 1998 it has been well established that runs which did not contribute to the pool are penalised when unjudged documents count as non-relevant. Sakai (SIGIR 2007) proposed condensed lists (drop unjudged documents, then score), and nDCG' is his metric. Sakai's own follow-up work warns that condensed-list metrics tend to **overestimate** runs that retrieve many unjudged documents. So the condensed-list gap shrinkage should be presented as bounding the artifact, not as the corrected true gap.

### Cited Findings
- **Sakai, "Alternatives to Bpref", SIGIR 2007, pp. 71–78** (DOI 10.1145/1277741.1277756) **[S]**. Proposes the condensed list: unjudged documents are removed from a ranking before computing standard metrics. The resulting metrics are Q′, AP′ and nDCG′. Sakai reports these are simpler than bpref and better at handling incomplete judgments, and that Q′, nDCG′ and AP′ outperformed bpref and RBP in his experiments. — [Crossref record](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1145%2F1277741.1277756); [SIGIR 2007 dblp](https://dblp.org/db/conf/sigir/sigir2007); [NTCIR EVIA 2007 related paper](https://research.nii.ac.jp/ntcir/workshop/OnlineProceedings6/EVIA/1.pdf)
- **Sakai, follow-up in Information and Media Technologies 4(2):547–557, 2009** (journal version of the "bias" study) **[S]**: "the condensed-list versions of AP, Q and nDCG are not necessarily superior to the original metrics for handling biases", although AP′ and Q′ remain generally superior to bpref, RBP and condensed RBP even under bias. — [J-STAGE](https://www.jstage.jst.go.jp/article/imt/4/2/4_2_547/_article/-char/en)
- **Sakai & Kando, "On information retrieval metrics designed for evaluation with incomplete relevance assessments", Information Retrieval 11(5), 2008** (DOI 10.1007/s10791-008-9059-7) **[S for existence; U for content]**. Springer redirected to a login, so the full text was not read. As I recall it, the paper finds that condensed-list metrics are more robust to incompleteness than bpref but can **overestimate** systems whose unjudged documents are actually non-relevant. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/doi/10.1007/s10791-008-9059-7)
- A search snippet (source paper not pinned down) summarises the literature this way: "condensed list-based measures often overestimate the effectiveness of a system" **[S, source unpinned]**. It is consistent with Sakai & Kando and with Yilmaz & Aslam's comparisons. — search result set incl. [NTCIR EVIA 2007 paper 11](https://research.nii.ac.jp/ntcir/workshop/OnlineProceedings6/EVIA/11.pdf)
- **Buckley, Dimmick, Soboroff, Voorhees, "Bias and the limits of pooling for large collections", Information Retrieval 10(6), 2007** **[S]**. Shows that TREC 2005 (AQUAINT, large collection) pools were biased toward relevant documents containing topic title words. A run that retrieves documents outside the pool has them treated as non-relevant. The authors also note that the mere presence of unjudged documents in a run does not prove its evaluation is unfair. — [Springer](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10791-007-9032-x); [NIST PDF](https://tsapps.nist.gov/publication/get_pdf.cfm?pub_id=51236)
- **Buckley & Voorhees, "Retrieval evaluation with incomplete information", SIGIR 2004** (bpref) **[S existence / U details]**. Introduces bpref, which uses only judged documents and is robust when judgments are incomplete. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/200110698_Retrieval_evaluation_with_incomplete_information)
- **Yilmaz & Aslam, "Estimating average precision with incomplete and imperfect judgments", CIKM 2006** (infAP) **[U]**. Random sampling within the pool gives an unbiased estimate of AP. Later work (Yilmaz, Kanoulas & Aslam, SIGIR 2008) extended this to infNDCG. — URL not retrieved; check the ACM DL.
- **Webber & Park, "Score adjustment for correction of pooling bias", SIGIR 2009**; **Webber, PhD thesis 2010** **[U]**. Adjust leave-one-run-out pooling bias.
- **Lipani, Lupu, Hanbury, "Splitting water: precision and anti-precision to reduce pool bias", SIGIR 2015**; **Lipani et al., "The curious incidence of bias corrections in the pool", ECIR 2016** **[U]**. Estimators that correct P@n for runs that did not contribute to the pool. A search result also lists a SIGIR 2007 ACM paper on reliable evaluation "with incomplete and biased judgements" **[S]** (Yilmaz & Aslam / Büttcher, Clarke, Yeung & Soboroff SIGIR 2007; author attribution not verified). — [ACM DL 10.1145/1277741.1277755](https://dl.acm.org/doi/pdf/10.1145/1277741.1277755)
- **Zobel, "How reliable are the results of large-scale information retrieval experiments?", SIGIR 1998** **[U]**. Leave-one-run-out experiment showing that TREC pools are reasonably reusable for runs that did not contribute, with a small systematic bias against them.
- **Voorhees, Soboroff, Lin, "Can old TREC collections reliably evaluate modern neural retrieval models?", arXiv 2201.11086 (2022)** **[U]**. Pools neural runs against Robust04 and adds new judgments. This is the closest "reusability for new paradigms" study a reviewer will cite. Also **Voorhees, Craswell, Lin, "Too many relevants: whither Cranfield test collections?", SIGIR 2022** **[U]**.

### Inferences
- The method itself (judged@10, condensed nDCG′ as a hole-robust variant) is textbook. A reviewer will cite Sakai 2007 and Sakai & Kando 2008. The paper's contribution is the **application to ANN-vs-exact**. There the "new system" is a near-copy of a pooled system, which is a particularly clean case of leave-one-run-out: ANN differs from exact only on the substituted documents.
- Because condensed-list metrics are known to overestimate runs that have holes, the "half to three-quarters" shrinkage is best framed as an upper bound on the pooling artifact. Pair it with a lower bound (holes treated as non-relevant, the standard) and, ideally, a midpoint (LLM fill-in of holes; see Q2).

### Gaps
- Full text of Sakai 2007 and Sakai & Kando 2008 was not retrieved (paywall/403), so no verbatim definition is quoted here. Pull the definition sentence from the ACM DL PDF before submission.
- Zobel 1998, infAP, Webber and Lipani details are from memory only.

## Q2. Recent work on holes for neural and dense retrievers (BEIR, MS MARCO/TREC DL, LLM hole-filling, judged@k)

### Takeaway
It is well known, and quantified, that dense retrievers have far higher hole rates than lexical ones against lexically biased pools, and that filling holes raises their nDCG substantially. BEIR's TREC-COVID study is the canonical reviewer citation, and it already reports a "Hole@10, then re-annotate, then nDCG rises" result for dense models. Filling holes with LLMs is also established (MacAvaney & Soldaini 2023; Upadhyay, Kamalloo & Lin 2024; Abbasiantaeb et al. 2024).

### Cited Findings
- **Thakur, Reimers, Rücklé, Srivastava, Gurevych, "BEIR", NeurIPS 2021 Datasets & Benchmarks (arXiv 2104.08663)** **[V]**. Verbatim: "A new retrieval system might retrieve vastly different results than the system used for the annotation. These hits are automatically assumed to be irrelevant." And: "Such a lexical bias disfavours approaches that don't rely on lexical matching, like dense retrieval methods, as retrieved hits without lexical overlap are automatically assumed to be irrelevant." Table 4 (TREC-COVID) gives Hole@10 rates: BM25 6.4%, DeepCT 19.4%, SPARTA 12.4%, docT5query 2.8%, DPR 30.6%, ANCE 14.4%, TAS-B 31.8%, ColBERT 12.4%, BM25+CE 1.6%. nDCG@10 before and after manual annotation of holes: BM25 0.656 to 0.668; DPR 0.332 to 0.445; ANCE 0.654 to 0.735; TAS-B 0.481 to 0.555; ColBERT 0.677 to 0.735; docT5query 0.713 to 0.714. Verbatim: "for the dense retrieval system ANCE, the performance improves from 0.654 (slightly below BM25) to 0.735 ... Even though many systems contributed to the TREC-COVID annotation pool, the annotation pool is still biased towards lexical approaches." In total 980 query-document pairs were annotated, blind to which system retrieved them. — [arXiv 2104.08663](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2104.08663)
- **Upadhyay, Kamalloo, Lin, "LLMs Can Patch Up Missing Relevance Judgments in Evaluation", arXiv 2405.04727 (2024)** **[V]**. "Unjudged documents or holes in information retrieval benchmarks are considered non-relevant in evaluation ... these missing judgments may inadvertently introduce biases into the evaluation." Different hole rates across systems make comparisons "unfair, particularly for models returning many unjudged documents." With 90% of judgments removed on TREC DL 2019/2020/2021 (nDCG@10), Kendall τ was GPT-3.5-Turbo 0.927/0.934/0.923 and Vicuña-7B 0.862/0.893/0.871, against 0.624/0.720/0.508 when holes count as non-relevant. — [ar5iv](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2405.04727)
- **Arabzadeh, Vtyurina, Yan, Clarke, "Shallow pooling for sparse labels", Information Retrieval Journal 2022 (arXiv 2109.00062)** **[S]**. In the MS MARCO passage dev set, 94% of queries have a single known relevant passage. In crowdsourced preference judgments, top results from neural rankers were often preferred over the "known" relevant passage. The authors conclude MS MARCO may no longer recognise genuine improvements. — [arXiv 2109.00062](https://arxiv.org/abs/2109.00062)
- **MacAvaney & Soldaini, "One-Shot Labeling for Automatic Relevance Estimation", SIGIR 2023 (arXiv 2302.11266)** **[U]**. Fills holes with one-shot LLM labels and reports better system-ranking correlation than treating holes as non-relevant.
- **Abbasiantaeb, Meng, Azzopardi, Aliannejadi, "Can We Use Large Language Models to Fill Relevance Judgment Holes?", arXiv 2405.05600 (2024)** **[U]**. Conversational-search setting (TREC iKAT). Finds that the source of hole labels (LLM vs human) matters for consistency.
- **Judged@k convention**: Anserini/Pyserini and ir_measures report "Judged@10" (the fraction of the top 10 that is judged) alongside nDCG@10 for MS MARCO/TREC DL dense runs **[U]**. Check the ir_measures docs (`Judged@10`) and the Pyserini reproduction pages.
- **Nelson et al., "Incremental Pooled LLM Evaluation for Cost-Effective Retrieval Model Selection", arXiv 2609.02745 (Sept 2026, preprint)** **[V abstract]**. Uses LLM judging of the pooled union of 11 dense, sparse and hybrid systems, and reports that "97% of pairwise system orderings are preserved". It does not discuss ANN or condensed lists. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.02745)

### Inferences
- A reviewer will say "BEIR showed dense runs have holes and gain after re-annotation". The paper needs to cite BEIR Table 4 and say exactly what is different. In BEIR the comparison is between **different models**; here it is between **the same model under exact vs approximate search**. Here the holes are concentrated precisely in the ANN-substituted documents, so the artifact acts directly on the efficiency/effectiveness trade-off measurement.
- The strongest defence of claim 5 is to fill the substituted holes with the same LLM judge (GPT-OSS-120B, as LongEval did) and report all three versions: holes as non-relevant, condensed, and LLM-filled. That follows Upadhyay–Kamalloo–Lin and MacAvaney–Soldaini, which reviewers will expect.

### Gaps
- MacAvaney & Soldaini, Abbasiantaeb et al., Voorhees et al. 2022 and the Pyserini judged@k text were not fetched this session.
- No MS MARCO or TREC DL "holes" numbers were found specifically for HNSW vs flat runs.

## Q3. Has anyone shown ANN/approximate search runs are disadvantaged by pools built from exact or other runs?

### Takeaway
I found **no** paper that measures hole rates or condensed-list effects for ANN-vs-exact runs of the same encoder. ANN-in-IR papers compare HNSW and brute force on the existing (sparse) qrels and treat the small nDCG/MRR drop as real loss. This appears to be the novel part of claim 5, with the caveat that absence of evidence comes from about 6 targeted searches.

### Cited Findings
- **Ma, Teofili, Lin et al., "Anserini Gets Dense Retrieval: Integration of Lucene's HNSW Indexes", CIKM 2023 (arXiv 2304.12139)** **[S]**. HNSW vs brute-force flat on MS MARCO. MRR@10 approaches exact search as efSearch grows (peak about 0.3842 at efSearch=1000). The effectiveness gap is reported on the standard qrels with no hole analysis. — [arXiv 2304.12139](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.12139)
- **Kulkarni, MacAvaney, Goharian, Frieder, "LADR: Lexically-Accelerated Dense Retrieval", SIGIR 2023 (arXiv 2307.16779)** **[S]**. Compares approximate dense retrieval against exhaustive search on TREC DL. MacAvaney's group normally reports Judged@10, but no ANN-specific pooling-bias conclusion was found. — [arXiv 2307.16779](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2307.16779)
- **Hofstätter et al., "Are We There Yet? A Decision Framework for Replacing Term Based Retrieval with Dense Retrieval Systems", ICTIR 2023 (arXiv 2206.12993)** **[S]**. Compares exhaustive search with HNSW-128 on TREC DL and treats the measured differences as real effectiveness loss. — [arXiv 2206.12993](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2206.12993)
- Searches combining "approximate nearest neighbor", "unjudged", "pooling bias" and "condensed" returned only classic pooling papers and ANN-benchmark material. ANN benchmarks use exact k-NN as ground truth (recall@k), not relevance judgments. — search results, e.g. [Buckley et al. NIST PDF](https://tsapps.nist.gov/publication/get_pdf.cfm?pub_id=51236)
- An unreliable aggregator page, "The Unjudged Document Problem in Retrieval Evaluation — Lacuna" (lacuna.tiptreesystems.com), appeared in results. It is not a primary source; do not cite it.

### Inferences
- Positioning: "ANN recall loss vs exact k-NN" (the vector-DB view) and "nDCG loss on qrels" (the IR view) are usually treated as interchangeable. The paper can argue that on pooled qrels a large part of the IR-view loss is unmeasurable, because the substituted documents were never judged. This bridges two communities and does not appear to have been published.
- Reviewer risk: someone may point out that this is a direct corollary of Zobel 1998 / BEIR. Pre-empt it by making the contribution quantitative and specific to ANN: the share of substituted documents that are unjudged, and gap shrinkage under condensed and LLM-filled hole treatments. Report it alongside recall@10 against exact.

### Gaps
- Not checked: the TREC DL / MS MARCO "Judged@10" tables in Pyserini for HNSW vs flat indexes. If they show identical Judged@10, that would weaken generality. Also not checked: whether the CIKM/SIGIR short-paper literature on quantization (PQ/OPQ, e.g. JPQ, RepCONC) discusses holes.

## Q4. Validity of LLM relevance judgments (Thomas et al., Faggioli, UMBRELA, Clarke & Dietz, Soboroff, TREC RAG 2024, LongEval)

### Takeaway
There is solid evidence that LLM labels preserve **system rankings** at run level (Thomas et al. 2024; UMBRELA / TREC RAG 2024). There are also serious, well-cited critiques: circularity when systems use LLMs, gameability, and LLMs being more lenient than humans (Clarke & Dietz; Soboroff). For the ANN paper the circularity risk is low, since neither the exact nor the ANN system uses an LLM ranker. The leniency bias matters, however: LLM judges label more documents relevant, which could inflate the credit given to substituted documents. LongEval 2026 creates LLM judgments from submission pools. No published organiser validation of those judgments (agreement statistics) was found.

### Cited Findings
- **Thomas, Spielman, Craswell, Mitra, "Large Language Models can Accurately Predict Searcher Preferences", SIGIR 2024, pp. 1930–1940, DOI 10.1145/3626772.3657707 (arXiv 2309.10621)** **[S]**. Uses real-searcher feedback as gold. LLM (GPT-4) labellers outperform several populations of human labellers, with crowd workers worst. LLM accuracy shifts with simple prompt paraphrases, so gold labels are still needed. — [arXiv 2309.10621](https://arxiv.org/abs/2309.10621); [dblp](https://dblp.org/rec/conf/sigir/0001SC024)
- **Faggioli et al., "Perspectives on Large Language Models for Relevance Judgment", ICTIR 2023 (arXiv 2304.09161)** **[S existence]**. A position paper setting out a spectrum of human–LLM collaboration and the risks of fully automatic judgments. — [arXiv 2304.09161](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.09161)
- **Upadhyay, Pradeep, Thakur, Craswell, Lin, "UMBRELA: UMbrela is the (Open-Source Reproduction of the) Bing RELevance Assessor", arXiv 2406.06519 (2024)** **[S]**. Reproduces Thomas et al.'s prompt. LLM labels correlate highly with run rankings on TREC DL 2019–2023. — [arXiv 2406.06519](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.06519v1)
- **Upadhyay, Pradeep, Thakur, Campos, Craswell, Soboroff, Dang, Lin, "A Large-Scale Study of Relevance Assessments with Large Language Models" (TREC 2024 RAG; arXiv 2411.08275; later version "Using UMBRELA", published 2025 per NIST)** **[S]**. 77 runs from 19 teams. Rankings under fully automatic UMBRELA judgments correlate well with fully manual ones on nDCG@20, nDCG@100 and Recall@100. LLM assistance in hybrid strategies did not increase correlation. Humans apply stricter criteria than UMBRELA, which is more lenient. — [arXiv 2411.08275](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.08275); [NIST](https://www.nist.gov/node/1897641)
- **Clarke & Dietz, "LLM-based relevance assessment still can't replace human relevance assessment", arXiv 2412.17156 (v2 prepared for EVIA 2025; v3 Jan 2026)** **[V]**. Verbatim: "there is no fundamental difference between an LLM-based relevance assessment and an LLM-based re-ranking method." Also "Once we adopt Umbrela as both a system component and an evaluation metric, it leads to an invalid circular evaluation." And the uwc1 run, "deliberately designed to subvert the automatic evaluation process", ranked "significantly higher under automatic assessment (5th) than under manual assessment (28th)." They invoke Goodhart's law. — [arXiv 2412.17156v2](https://arxiv.org/html/2412.17156v2)
- **Soboroff, "Don't Use LLMs to Make Relevance Judgments", Information Retrieval Research Journal 2025 (arXiv 2409.15133)** **[U]**. Argues that LLM judgments cannot measure systems that are better than the judge.
- **Alaofi et al., "LLMs can be Fooled into Labelling a Document as Relevant", SIGIR-AP 2024** **[U]**. Shows keyword-stuffing and adversarial vulnerability of LLM judges.
- **LongEval 2025 overview (Cancellieri et al., arXiv 2509.17469; CLEF 2025 LNCS / CEUR Vol-4038)** **[V]**. Both WebRetrieval and SciRetrieval qrels came from click models: "computed relevance assessments based on a simplified Dynamic Bayesian Network (sDBN) Click Model", and for Sci "based on click signals from user logs (e.g., PDF downloads)". The overview does not mention LLM-generated qrels or agreement statistics. — [arXiv 2509.17469](https://arxiv.org/html/2509.17469v1)
- A CEUR 2025 participant notebook **[S]** reports that the organisers released corrected training queries and qrels on 5 May 2025 and that remaining qrels issues persisted. It also says the click labels were "scheduled to be complemented with explicit human relevance assessments after the official" evaluation. — [CEUR Vol-4038 paper 271](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4038/paper_271.pdf)
- **LongEval 2024 extended overview (CEUR Vol-3740)** **[S]**. A manual annotation effort produced 25,759 judgments, with a more balanced relevant/non-relevant split than earlier editions. This is precedent for LongEval cross-checking click labels against humans. — [CEUR Vol-3740 paper 213](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-3740/paper-213.pdf)
- **LongEval 2026 task page** **[V]**. Task 1 (Sci): "after submission, LLM judgments will be created for pools from the submitted runs", with test data including "LLM judgments based on submission pools" and "Pseudo Qrles per snapshot (document click through rate and raw clicks)". Task 2 (TopEx) plans to validate LLM judgments via "agreement with click-log qrels", "ability to separate runs via nDCG", and "how consist are the annotations across different large language models". The page names no model (no GPT-OSS-120B/20B mention) and no pool depth. — [clef-longeval.github.io/tasks](https://clef-longeval.github.io/tasks/)
- **ir-datasets-longeval** **[S]**. The 2026 LongEval-Sci collection has three snapshots (March–November 2025), each with raw qrels (any click counts as relevant) and DCTR qrels. — [GitHub](https://github.com/clef-longeval/ir-datasets-longeval/tree/main)
- **CLEF 2026 Working Notes, CEUR Vol-4283** **[S]**. Includes the OpenWebSearch paper "Large Language Model Relevance Judgments as Relevance Feedback for Scientific Search". There is also arXiv 2607.04088 "LongEval-Sci at CLEF 2026", a participant paper using DCTR qrels as primary and raw qrels as sensitivity, and arXiv 2607.04008 (LongEval-RAG), where a supplementary LLM-judge evaluation ranks systems differently from the primary metrics. — [CEUR Vol-4283](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4283/); [arXiv 2607.04088](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.04088v1); [arXiv 2607.04008](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.04008)
- **gpt-oss as a judge**: arXiv 2604.04140, "Formalized Information Needs Improve Large-Language-Model Relevance Judgments" (2026), uses gpt-oss-120b as an assessor on TREC Robust04 (the Thomas et al. setting) and compares against TREC judgments **[S]**. This is useful independent evidence on GPT-OSS-120B judging quality. — [arXiv 2604.04140](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2604.04140)

### Inferences
- Pooling issue for the paper's own setup: if the LongEval GPT-OSS-120B judgments were built from **submission pools** (and the submitted runs were mostly exact or lexical), the LLM labels have the same holes as human pools. Using an LLM judge does not remove pooling bias unless the paper judges the ANN-substituted documents itself. This is the strongest reviewer objection to anticipate, and also the cleanest experiment to add: judge the holes with GPT-OSS-120B using LongEval's prompt.
- The sensitivity design (20B vs 120B, click raw/DCTR, 29 human queries) matches LongEval's own planned validation axes: cross-LLM consistency, agreement with click qrels, and run separation. Cite the task page for that alignment.
- Cite both sides: Thomas et al. / UMBRELA-TREC RAG (ranking fidelity) and Clarke & Dietz / Soboroff (limits). Argue that circularity does not apply because neither compared system uses an LLM. Leniency (UMBRELA is more lenient than humans) could inflate the condensed or filled gains, so report the 29 human-judged queries as the check.
- 29 queries is small. Report rank correlation and agreement with confidence intervals, not just point estimates.

### Gaps
- No LongEval 2026 overview with organiser-reported agreement statistics for the GPT-OSS-120B judgments was found. The task page does not name the model or the pool depth. Confirm the model, prompt and pool depth from the released qrels README or the 2026 overview (CLEF 2026 LNCS / CEUR Vol-4283) before citing.
- Not fetched this session: Faggioli et al. full text, Soboroff 2025, Alaofi 2024, and the LLMJudge/SynDL benchmarks (Rahmani et al. 2024).
- arXiv 2607.04088 uses a DCTR-primary / raw-sensitivity design. Check whether it overlaps with the paper's own design or authors.
