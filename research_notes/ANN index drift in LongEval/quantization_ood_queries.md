# Quantization staleness under drift, OOD queries for ANN, and label-free query-shift signals

Scope: novelty test for two proposal claims. (1) Compressed vectors (SQ/PQ) fitted to early data lose precision as data drifts, and refitting in place is a cheap maintenance step. (2) A label-free query-drift signal: median(query -> nearest-neighbour distance) / median(data row -> nearest-neighbour distance), used to flag that recall calibrated on data rows overstates recall on real queries.

## Q1. Quantization / codebook staleness under data distribution shift

### Takeaway
Stale quantizers under drift are a known, studied problem with dedicated prior work: Online PQ (TKDE 2018), DeDrift (ICCV 2023), CoDEQ (arXiv Dec 2025) and IVF-TQ (arXiv May 2026). The strongest measured evidence (DeDrift) says the **coarse IVF partition** is what breaks under drift (5-11 points of recall), while **PQ codebooks are robust (<1 point)**. One 2026 preprint finds per-batch PQ retraining gives no measurable recall benefit. So "refit the codebook in place" is not novel as an idea, and the claim that refitting recovers much precision is not supported by the literature for PQ. Calling it a cheap maintenance step has precedent (DeDrift-Lazy, 70-100x cheaper than a rebuild). I found nothing specific on scalar-quantization range clipping under drift. That narrow case (per-dimension min/max or percentile ranges going stale) seems to be the least-covered piece.

### Cited Findings
- **Online Product Quantization** (Donna Xu, Ivor W. Tsang, Ying Zhang; IEEE TKDE 2018; arXiv 1711.10775). It updates the PQ codebook incrementally as streaming data arrives, uses two budget constraints so only part of the codebook is updated, proves a loss bound, and supports a sliding window with insertions and deletions. Reported as time-efficient and effective against baselines on dynamic databases. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.com/abs/1711.10775); [UTS OPUS](https://opus.lib.uts.edu.au/handle/10453/125472)
- **DeDrift: Robust Similarity Search under Content Drift** (Baranchuk, Douze, Upadhyay, Yalniz; ICCV 2023). It studies real temporal drift on media sites (VideoAds, YFCC), where both the queries and the database shift over time. The protocol uses the most recent content as queries against a backlog of older content. — [arXiv 2308.02752](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.02752)
  - Measured recall loss from drift, ID -> OOD at 6000 distance computations: VideoAds IVF16384 0.842 -> 0.732 (about 11 points). YFCC IVF4096 0.796 -> 0.744 (about 5.2 points). VideoAds with OPQ32 residual PQ 0.587 -> 0.548 (about 3.9 points). — [ar5iv 2308.02752](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2308.02752)
  - **PQ codecs on their own are robust to drift: less than 1% recall loss in exhaustive search (their Table 1).** The authors attribute this to small codebooks that "can hardly fit the data". The **IVF coarse quantizer is the component that degrades**: clusters become progressively unbalanced (measured as entropy) as the time gap grows. — [ar5iv 2308.02752](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2308.02752)
  - Fixes: DeDrift-Lazy recomputes centroids without reassigning vectors. DeDrift-Split re-splits the largest clusters and merges the smallest. A hybrid does both. For PQ, Lazy has to keep old centroid versions so lookup tables stay correct. Cost: full rebuild about 430 s (VideoAds) or about 30 s (YFCC), against DeDrift-Lazy about 24 s or about 8 s, described as "up to 100x" (VideoAds) and about 70x (YFCC) faster. Almost all of the accuracy loss is recovered. — [ar5iv 2308.02752](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2308.02752); [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.02752)
- **Quantization for Vector Search under Streaming Updates** (Aden-Ali, Ferhatosmanoglu, Greaves-Tunnell, Mishra, Wagner; arXiv 2512.18335, 20 Dec 2025). It proposes **CoDEQ**, a data-dependent quantizer that is "provably dynamically consistent", meaning it adapts as the dataset evolves. Tested on 100M-scale DEEP (96d), BigANN (128d) and Text2Image (200d). The "FrozenPQ" baseline loses recall as the stream progresses, and loses more when queries favour recent data. Query freshness is a parameter α: α=1 means queries come from the newest cluster, α=0 means uniform over everything seen. CoDEQ "maintains a roughly constant level of recall despite the drifting updates and queries". Heuristic OnlinePQ falls behind RebuildPQ "due to lack of dynamic consistency". SQ appears only as a data-oblivious example in theory (Appendix A), with no SQ experiments. The extracted text did not include exact recall-loss numbers. — [arXiv 2512.18335](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.18335); [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.18335)
- **IVF-TQ: Calibration-Free Streaming Vector Search via a Codebook-Free Residual Layer** (Tarun Sharma, independent researcher; arXiv 2605.17415v2, May 2026; not peer reviewed). It argues that streaming staleness in PQ is mainly a **capacity** problem, not a distribution-shift problem. Under shuffled i.i.d. ingestion with no shift at all, PQ still loses about 3.8 points at sub-matched bit budgets. IVF-PQ streaming degradation is -2.3 to -4.2 points across 3 datasets (10M) x 3 memory regimes x 3 seeds. **Per-batch codebook retraining is statistically indistinguishable from no retraining in 8 of 9 cells**, and costs 667±20 s (Deep-10M), 821±7 s (SIFT-10M) and 1328±43 s (T2I-10M) of cumulative compute per run. Under a harsh embedding-model swap (cosine 0.24), the frozen data-oblivious IVF-TQ (random rotation plus Lloyd-Max SQ) beats actively retrained IVF-PQ by +14.72±0.43 points. It also notes that coordinates drifting off the codebook grid waste centroids, and proposes per-coordinate calibration to map them back. — [arXiv 2605.17415](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.17415v2)
- Data-oblivious quantizers sidestep staleness. RaBitQ's preprocessing (a random rotation) is "frozen" and data-independent, which CoDEQ lists as a related-work contrast. — [arXiv 2512.18335 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.18335). The streaming SQ in IVF-TQ is parameterized only by bit-width and dimension. — [arXiv 2605.17415](https://arxiv.org/html/2605.17415v2)
- SPFresh is cited as a related streaming method (approximately balanced reclustering heuristics). — [arXiv 2512.18335 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2512.18335)

### Inferences
- Claim (1) in its general form ("compressed codes fitted early go stale; refit in place") has clear prior art: Online PQ 2018, DeDrift 2023, CoDEQ 2025. Novelty has to come from something narrower. Candidates: scalar quantization with fitted per-dimension ranges (int8/SQ8 min-max or percentile clipping), which none of these papers measures; the HNSW (non-IVF) setting; or a measured cost/benefit in a specific product setting such as LongEval temporal snapshots.
- The evidence argues against a large precision gain from refitting PQ (DeDrift <1% PQ loss; IVF-TQ finds no benefit from retraining). For SQ the mechanism is different. Values outside a stale [min, max] get clipped, which could cause larger, outlier-driven errors. That is plausible but unmeasured in what I found, so the proposal should measure it rather than assume it.
- Under a strong shift (a model swap), data-oblivious methods (RaBitQ-style rotation plus fixed SQ) may make the refit unnecessary. Reviewers will likely ask "why not RaBitQ?".

### Gaps
- No source found that quantifies recall loss from stale **scalar-quantization ranges** (SQ8/int8 min-max or percentile clipping) under drift.
- I did not reach the FAISS wiki's guidance on retraining IVF-PQ or the size and representativeness of the training set. It was not verified in this pass.
- No explicit drift-robustness study found for extended RaBitQ. Its robustness is inferred from it being data-oblivious, not from a measured drift experiment.
- Exact CoDEQ recall numbers were not extracted (they are in its Figures 5-6).
- I found no learned-quantizer-under-shift papers beyond these.

## Q2. OOD queries for ANN: indexes built or calibrated on base data underperform on OOD queries

### Takeaway
It is well established, and quantified, that queries from a different distribution than the base data (text -> image, cross-modal) cost far more search effort at the same recall. The figures are an order of magnitude or more in latency (OOD-DiskANN) and about 10x the hops in HNSW (RoarGraph). VIBE (2025) shows that in-distribution benchmarks overstate performance on OOD workloads. The overstatement half of the proposal's claim is therefore known. What may be new is detecting it label-free at run time inside a product (see Q3).

### Cited Findings
- **OOD-DiskANN** (Jaiswal, Krishnaswamy, Garg, Simhadri, Agrawal; arXiv 2211.12850; The Web Conference 2023). At a fixed recall target, latency for OOD queries is worse "by an order of magnitude or more" than for ID queries. Example: an image-embedding index queried with text embeddings. Building the graph with a sample of OOD queries equal to 1% of the index size gives up to 40% lower mean latency. — [arXiv 2211.12850](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.12850)
- **RoarGraph** (Chen et al.; PVLDB 17(11), 2024; arXiv 2408.08933). The kNN of an OOD query lie far from each other, which breaks the assumptions of graph indexes. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.08933v1)
  - OOD quantified with 2-Wasserstein distance. Text-to-Image W2(B1,Q)=1.439 vs W2(B1,B2)=0.864. LAION 1.677 vs 0.882. WebVid 1.683 vs 0.581. So queries sit 1.67x, 1.89x and 2.89x farther from the base data than one base sample sits from another. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.08933v1)
  - **"OOD queries are 2.1 times, 5.3 times, and 11.3 times farther from their nearest neighbors than ID queries"** (T2I, LAION, WebVid). For k=100, the distances among an OOD query's neighbours are about 1.29x, 1.45x and 2.11x those of an ID query. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.08933v1)
  - In HNSW on LAION, OOD queries need more than 500 hops to reach recall@10 ≥ 0.93, against 48 hops for ID queries (about 10x). RoarGraph is up to 3.56x faster at 90% recall on OOD queries. — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.08933v1); [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2408.08933)
- **VIBE: Vector Index Benchmark for Embeddings** (Ceccarello, Aumüller et al.; arXiv 2505.17810, May 2025). It evaluates 21 implementations on 12 ID and 6 OOD datasets (4 text-to-image, 2 from attention computation). It reports separate breakdowns for easy/hard and ID/OOD queries. Query difficulty is measured with relative contrast (mean distance to the dataset divided by distance to the k-th NN). OOD-ness is visualized with Mahalanobis-distance distributions and PCA. Regular methods degrade sharply on OOD text-to-image data. On the attention datasets they struggle to reach 50% recall, while OOD-specialized methods succeed. — [arXiv 2505.17810](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.17810); [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.17810v1)
- DeDrift's temporal protocol (recent queries against older content) found 5-11 points of recall loss for IVF at a fixed budget. This is an OOD effect that comes from time rather than modality. — [ar5iv 2308.02752](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2308.02752)

### Inferences
- Calibrating recall or ef on base rows (or on held-out base samples treated as queries) and reporting that number to users is exactly the bias VIBE and RoarGraph show. The proposal should cite them as motivation, not claim the finding as its own.
- The temporal OOD case (LongEval-style queries drifting over months) is less studied for ANN than the cross-modal case. DeDrift is the main exception.

### Gaps
- Not checked in this pass: Yandex T2I and LAION per-algorithm numbers from big-ann-benchmarks (NeurIPS'21/'23 OOD track), and newer OOD graph methods from 2025-2026 beyond RoarGraph and OOD-DiskANN.

## Q3. Label-free detection of query shift and query difficulty; does the query-NN / row-NN distance ratio exist in prior work?

### Takeaway
The ingredients are well established. Distance to the nearest neighbour is the standard non-parametric OOD score in ML (Sun et al., ICML 2022, "KNN"). RoarGraph reports exactly the ratio "OOD query NN distance / ID query NN distance" (2.1x / 5.3x / 11.3x) as **descriptive evidence** that queries are OOD. VIBE, Aumüller and Ceccarello, and Steiner-hardness provide per-query difficulty measures (RC, LID, expansion, Steiner-hardness). **I found no prior work that defines median(query->NN distance) / median(row->NN distance) as a named, run-time, label-free monitoring signal in a vector index, or that ties it to "recall calibrated on rows overstates recall on queries".** The closest analogues are RoarGraph's reported ratio (offline and descriptive) and KNN-OOD (a per-sample threshold calibrated on ID data). The defensible novelty claim is the operational use: an index-side health metric with a threshold, linked to recall-calibration bias, with no relevance judgements. The underlying statistic is not new.

### Cited Findings
- **Out-of-Distribution Detection with Deep Nearest Neighbors** (Yiyou Sun, Yifei Ming, Xiaojin Zhu, Yixuan Li; ICML 2022, PMLR 162). The OOD score is the k-th NN distance from a test embedding to the training embeddings, with a threshold. It is "distributional assumption free" and "OOD-agnostic (the threshold is estimated on ID data only)". Calibrating the threshold on ID data is conceptually equivalent to normalizing by the data's own NN distances. — [PMLR](https://proceedings.mlr.press/v162/sun22d.html); [arXiv 2204.06507](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.06507v3)
- **RoarGraph** directly compares query-to-NN distances between OOD and ID queries (2.1x, 5.3x, 11.3x), and measures OOD-ness with Wasserstein and Mahalanobis distances. It is used to motivate the design, not as a run-time detector. — [arXiv 2408.08933 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2408.08933v1)
- **Steiner-hardness** (Zeyu Wang, Qitong Wang, Xiaoxing Cheng, Peng Wang, Themis Palpanas, Wei Wang; PVLDB 17(13):4668-4682, 2024). A graph-native, connection-based hardness measure: the minimum search effort on a representative graph, computed by reduction to Directed Steiner Tree. It correlates with actual query effort much better than LID and other distance-based measures. They use it to build unbiased, hardness-graded workloads, and this changes the robustness rankings of graph indexes. — [PVLDB PDF](https://vldb.org/pvldb/vol17/p4668-wang.pdf); [arXiv 2408.13899](https://www.emergentmind.com/papers/2408.13899)
- **The Role of Local Intrinsic Dimensionality in Benchmarking Nearest Neighbor Search** (Aumüller and Ceccarello; SISAP 2019, extended in Information Systems 2021). LID, local relative contrast and query expansion can be used to pick query sets that span a wide range of difficulty. Real-world datasets are "not diverse": results on one dataset predict results on the others well. — [arXiv 1907.07387](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1907.07387v1.pdf); [Inf. Syst. PDF](https://www.research.unipd.it/retrieve/901e85da-9f3c-4cd9-9202-a8561418867c/1-s2.0-S0306437921000569-main.pdf)
- **VIBE** defines relative contrast as mean query-to-data distance divided by distance to the k-th NN, and uses Mahalanobis-distance distributions (corpus-corpus against corpus-query) to characterize OOD-ness. That is a distributional comparison of query-side and data-side distances, close in spirit to the proposed ratio, but based on Mahalanobis distance rather than NN distance, and used for visualization rather than as a scalar alarm. — [arXiv 2505.17810 HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.17810v1)
- **Dense QPP**: "On Coherence-based Predictors for Dense Query Performance Prediction" (Vlachou and Macdonald; arXiv 2310.11405). It adapts unsupervised coherence-based predictors to dense retrievers (ANCE, TCT-ColBERT) using embeddings of the retrieved set. Query type explains up to 35% of the instability in QPP predictors, a sensitivity unique to dense retrieval. This is label-free effectiveness prediction, but per query and about relevance, not about ANN recall. — [arXiv 2310.11405](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2310.11405); [Glasgow eprints](https://eprints.gla.ac.uk/328868)
- Hubness: not covered by the sources retrieved in this pass (gap).

### Inferences
- Suggested positioning: "We use a scale-free, label-free ratio of query-side to data-side nearest-neighbour distances, related to KNN OOD scoring [Sun et al. 2022] and to RoarGraph's observation that OOD queries sit 2-11x farther from their neighbours, as an operational alarm that row-calibrated recall is optimistic." Do not claim the statistic itself is new.
- RoarGraph's numbers give a ready external sanity scale: ratios of about 2x (T2I) to about 11x (WebVid) correspond to severe ANN slowdowns. The proposal can check whether its ratio reproduces values of that order on these public datasets.
- A known weakness to anticipate: NN-distance ratios measure geometric OOD-ness, while Steiner-hardness shows distance-based measures (LID-like) correlate only moderately with graph search effort. The proposal should report how well the ratio correlates with the actual recall gap, not just whether it detects shift.

### Gaps
- I could not run an exhaustive search ruling out a 2025-2026 paper that names this exact median ratio. The statement "not found" rests on about 15 searches and fetches, not a systematic review.
- Hubness-based query-shift detection for ANN was not covered.
- Embedding-drift monitoring in industry (MMD, Wasserstein, centroid shift) appears only in blogs or secondary sources and was not used as evidence.

## Q4. Adaptive / early-termination search calibrated per query, and calibration under shift

### Takeaway
Learned early termination (LAET, SIGMOD 2020) and declarative-recall early termination (DARTH, PACMMOD/SIGMOD 2025-26) predict per-query recall from features of the search state, mostly NN distances found so far. DARTH tests explicitly against noisy (harder) queries and OOD (T2I-100M) queries and stays close to target, while LAET has more queries miss. This is the closest prior art to "calibration that adapts to query shift". These methods are trained on learn-set queries, not labelled relevance, and they correct recall per query instead of detecting shift globally.

### Cited Findings
- **DARTH: Declarative Recall Through Early Termination for ANN Search** (Manos Chatzakis, Yannis Papakonstantinou, Themis Palpanas; arXiv 2505.19001; Proc. ACM Manag. Data 3(4), 2025; SIGMOD 2026 poster). The user states a target recall, and a GBDT (LightGBM) recall predictor decides when HNSW search stops. It uses 11 features: n_step, n_dis, n_inserts, firstNN, closestNN, k-th NN distance, and mean/var/median/percentiles of NN distances. n_step, closestNN, firstNN, n_inserts and var account for about 74% of feature importance. It is trained on 10K learn-set queries (not base vectors or test queries), giving 43M-160M observations, in 85-155 s. Speedups are up to 14.6x (mean 6.8x, median 5.7x) over no early termination. With 12% Gaussian noise at R_t=0.90, only 13% of queries fall below target against 22-28% for competitors, and RDE is 94% better than LAET. On OOD T2I-100M it stays above the declared target and is 47% better than LAET in RQUT. Under harder conditions LAET's minimum per-query recall is 0.46, against more than 0.80 for DARTH. LAET needs per-target tuning, and DARTH answers on average 6K (up to 10K) queries before LAET finishes tuning. — [arXiv 2505.19001](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.19001v1); [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.19001); [project page](https://mchatzakis.github.io/declarative-recall/)
- **LAET** (Li et al., "Improving Approximate Nearest Neighbor Search through Learned Adaptive Early Termination", SIGMOD 2020). Characterized here only through DARTH's comparison (per-target multiplier tuning, weaker under hard and OOD queries). The primary paper was not fetched. — [via DARTH HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.19001)

### Inferences
- DARTH's top features (firstNN and closestNN distances) are query-side NN distances, the numerator of the proposed ratio. That supports the ratio as a meaningful signal. It also means a reviewer may argue that per-query adaptive termination already handles the "recall overstated" problem. The proposal's distinct angle is (a) a global, label-free alarm that **reported or calibrated recall** is biased, as opposed to adjusting search effort per query, and (b) zero training.
- DARTH's predictor is trained on learn-set queries. If production queries drift away from the learn set, the predictor itself may go stale. The proposed ratio could also serve as a trigger to retrain such predictors. This is an untested idea.

### Gaps
- The primary LAET paper (Li, Zhang, Andersen, He; SIGMOD 2020) was not fetched for its own numbers.
- No study found that measures how DARTH- or LAET-style predictors degrade when the training queries are base rows rather than real queries. That would be the direct test of the "row-calibrated recall overstates" claim.
