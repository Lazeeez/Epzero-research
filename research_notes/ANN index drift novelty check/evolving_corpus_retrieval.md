# Evolving-corpus retrieval: does prior work measure how ANN index error changes over time? (adversarial novelty check)

Proposal under test: in longitudinal IR evaluation, the ANN index's own error may grow as the collection evolves (incremental inserts, stale quantization, stale calibration, query shift) and be mistaken for model/data drift. The plan is to split effectiveness change into an exact-search component and an index component, and to compare rebuild, incremental update and in-place maintenance across time snapshots.

Search effort: 12 web searches and 13 page fetches (2026-11-12). Several PDFs (ACL Anthology, CEUR) could not be parsed by the fetch tool, so some claims rest on abstracts or search snippets. Those are marked.

Overall verdict: I found **no work that subsumes the proposal**. The literature splits into two groups that do not overlap:
(a) **Effectiveness-over-time work** (continual dense/generative retrieval, temporal benchmarks, LongEval-style snapshots). It reports nDCG, Hit@k or answer accuracy against labels, but uses exhaustive search or does not separate out the ANN index.
(b) **Streaming-ANN systems work** (FreshDiskANN, IP-DiskANN, streaming quantization, HNSW real-time updates). It measures index decay under updates, but only as k-NN recall against exact neighbours, never as relevance-labelled effectiveness across real temporal snapshots.
The closest single item is a Spring 2026 student presentation on ANN under query drift (k-NN recall only).

## Q1. Continual/incremental dense retrieval and backward-compatible embeddings: does any of it measure ANN index error over time?

### Takeaway
Continual dense-retrieval work measures effectiveness drift and tries to avoid re-encoding or re-indexing. Where the search method is stated, it is exhaustive. None of it separates ANN error from model error. BCT-style work removes the need to backfill embeddings, but it does not study the ANN structure.

### Cited Findings
- **GradNormIR**: "When Should Dense Retrievers Be Updated in Evolving Corpora? Detecting Out-of-Distribution Corpora Using GradNormIR" (Findings of ACL 2025; arXiv 2506.01877). It predicts whether an incoming corpus is OOD for the retriever *before indexing*, so the retriever is retrained only when needed. It notes that "indexing documents that dense retrievers fail to generalize to can severely degrade retrieval performance in evolving corpora." The drift it targets is model/corpus drift, not ANN error. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.1334.pdf), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2506.01877). Verdict: **different, but adjacent.** It is a model-update trigger. Whether it uses ANN or exact search was not verified (PDF not parsed).
- **L²R**: Cai et al., "L²R: Lifelong Learning for First-stage Retrieval with Backward-Compatible Representations", CIKM 2023 (arXiv 2308.11512). It uses a memory mechanism and a ranking-alignment objective so representations stay backward compatible, "to save the cost of index rebuilding without hurting model performance." It builds new drift benchmarks from LoTTE and Multi-CPR. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.11512v1), [CIKM PDF](https://jiafengguo.github.io/2023/CIKM2023-L2R.pdf). Verdict: **partial overlap on framing** (it avoids re-indexing on an evolving corpus). Its "index" means embeddings, not the ANN structure. ANN usage and any exact-vs-ANN split were not verified from the full text.
- **BCT**: Shen et al., "Towards Backward-Compatible Representation Learning", CVPR 2020. An influence loss makes new-model embeddings comparable with the old gallery, so no backfilling is needed. — [CVF](https://openaccess.thecvf.com/content_CVPR_2020/papers/Shen_Towards_Backward-Compatible_Representation_Learning_CVPR_2020_paper.pdf), [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2003.11942). Its follow-ups include FCT, forward-compatible training with side information ([arXiv 2112.02805](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2112.02805)), and boundary-aware BCT ([arXiv 2305.02610](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2305.02610)). Verdict: **different.** These works address embedding-space compatibility after a model update. The corpus is static, and the evaluation is image retrieval accuracy, with no ANN index or temporal snapshots.
- **AURORA**: Saravanan, Salla, Amancherla, "AURORA: Neuro-Symbolic Continual Indexing for Evolving RAG Systems", Findings of ACL 2026 (2026.findings-acl.495). It states that "most retrieval infrastructure assumes a stationary query document distribution after index construction" and that this "often fails" under continual knowledge updates. It decouples the discrete index structure from continuous metric representations, and uses Bayesian routing between adaptive and static indices. It reports up to +26.9% Recall@10 on novel topics against static baselines, and 28 ms adaptation against 5.1 s for full retraining, across dense, SPLADE and DSI settings. — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.495/), per the search-result abstract. Verdict: **partially overlaps — the closest in spirit on the effectiveness side.** It treats index maintenance under distribution shift as a first-class problem and compares adaptation with retraining. Based on the abstract only, its "index" is a learned retrieval index or metric adapter, not ANN graph or quantization error. It proposes a method rather than a decomposition, and it does not report an exact-vs-ANN split. The full text could not be parsed, so **the full paper should be read before claiming novelty against it.**
- **RUDI**: Althammer et al., "RUDI: Real-Time Learning to Update Dense Retrieval Indices", CEUR-WS Vol-2950 (DESIRES 2021). Judging from the title and venue, it concerns updating dense index embeddings when the encoder changes. — [CEUR](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-2950/paper-20.pdf). The PDF was not parseable, so its content is unverified. Verdict: **likely different** (encoder-update and embedding-refresh cost, not ANN decay).
- **"Embedding Surgery"**: arXiv 2609.05110. It makes localized embedding updates and reports nDCG@10 and Recall@100 on MS MARCO/DL before and after the updates. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2609.05110). This comes from a search snippet only. Verdict: **different** (ranking correction, not evolving-corpus ANN error).
- **ANCE**: Xiong et al., ICLR 2021. It notes that keeping the ANN index fresh needs re-inference plus a rebuild, and that it is refreshed every m batches. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2007.00808v2). Verdict: **different** (staleness of the training-time negatives index, not test-time effectiveness).

### Inferences
- The continual dense-retrieval line treats "the index" as *the set of document embeddings*. Its concern is the cost of re-encoding. The ANN data structure (graph or quantizer) is treated as an exact oracle or ignored. This is the blind spot the proposal targets.

### Gaps
- Not verified from full text: whether L²R, GradNormIR or AURORA use FAISS-HNSW/IVF-PQ or exact search, and whether any of them reports a flat-vs-ANN ablation over time. None of the abstracts mentions such an ablation. This is absence of evidence, not evidence of absence.

## Q2. Generative retrieval with incremental indexing (DSI++, IncDSI, CLEVER and others): are dense+ANN baselines updated incrementally vs rebuilt, and is the gap reported?

### Takeaway
GR-on-dynamic-corpora papers compare against dual encoders, but the one paper verified in detail uses brute-force search for the dual encoder. That removes ANN error by design rather than measuring it. CLEVER's incremental PQ is the nearest technical analogue to "stale quantization", but it is used for docids, not ANN search.

### Cited Findings
- **Kim, Yoon, Lee, Jang, Yang, Seo**, "Exploring the Practicality of Generative Retrieval on Dynamic Corpora", EMNLP 2024 (arXiv 2305.18952). The setup is StreamingQA (50M passages, 2007–2020), with indexing-based updates and training-based updates, measured by Hit@k and AnswerRecall@k. It states: "for DE, we employ the exhaustive (brute-force) search method adopted by our baselines". It notes that approximate search introduces "a trade-off between speed and accuracy" but does not measure it. It also says DE "necessitates re-indexing the entire corpus each time whenever the model is updated." — [arXiv HTML v5](https://arxiv.org/html/2305.18952v5), [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.755.pdf). Verdict: **different, and useful evidence for the gap.** The closest GR paper explicitly sidesteps ANN error.
- **CLEVER**: Chen et al., "Continual Learning for Generative Retrieval over Dynamic Corpora" (CIKM 2023; arXiv 2308.14968). It proposes Incremental Product Quantization, in which two adaptive thresholds update only a subset of centroids while centroid indices stay fixed. It argues that dense retrieval should adapt "with the unlabeled new-coming documents, and avoid re-inferring all embeddings of old documents." — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.14968). Verdict: **partial overlap on mechanism** (stale vs partially updated PQ codebooks on a growing corpus). The PQ codes are docids for generation, not an ANN search quantizer, and there is no exact-search reference decomposition. Whether its DR baselines use ANN was not verified.
- **DSI++**: Mehta et al. (arXiv 2212.09744; EMNLP 2023). It uses sharpness-aware minimization and generative replay for incremental indexing in DSI. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2212.09744). Verdict: **different** (forgetting in model parameters).
- Survey coverage lists DSI++, IncDSI, CLEVER and CorpusBrain++ as incremental-GR solutions (replay, constrained optimization, IPQ, continual pre-training). — [ACM TOIS survey](https://dl.acm.org/doi/full/10.1145/3722552). Newer work in this line includes MixLoRA-DSI ([arXiv 2507.09924](https://arxiv.org/html/2507.09924)) and a Parametric Memory Head for continual GR (Mekonnen, de Rijke et al. 2026; [arXiv 2604.23388](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.23388v1)). Verdict: **different** (model-side forgetting). Their dense-baseline index handling was not verified.

### Inferences
- When incremental-GR papers report "DE degradation on new documents", that degradation folds together encoder generalization and, if ANN is used, index error. Because the verified paper uses brute force, DE drops reported there are pure model/data effects. This supports the proposal's premise that the index component has gone unmeasured.

### Gaps
- IncDSI and CorpusBrain++ baseline index configurations were not checked.

## Q3. Time-sensitive RAG/QA benchmarks with live or updated indexes: does any study index maintenance strategy vs effectiveness?

### Takeaway
Temporal benchmarks (StreamingQA, FreshStack drift, FreshQA and others) study how content and knowledge change. They do not compare ANN maintenance strategies or isolate index error. Practitioner and grey literature asserts that HNSW degrades as the collection grows, but this is not peer-reviewed longitudinal evidence.

### Cited Findings
- **StreamingQA**: Liška et al., 2022 (arXiv 2205.11388). 50M+ passages over 14 years with temporal metadata, built for adaptation to new knowledge. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.11388). Verdict: **different** (a benchmark that could host the proposal's experiments).
- **"Still Fresh? Evaluating Temporal Drift in Retrieval Benchmarks"**: Kuissi, Subrahmanyan, Thakur, Lin, arXiv 2603.04532 (2026). It uses FreshStack snapshots from Oct 2024 and Oct 2025 and BM25, BGE-Gemma2, E5-Mistral, Qwen3 and fusion, measured with α-nDCG@10, Coverage@20, Recall@50 and Kendall τ. Scores fell between snapshots for most models. It does **not** use ANN indexes, does not compare incremental vs rebuild, and does not decompose error. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2603.04532). Verdict: **partial overlap on the longitudinal-evaluation framing, and different on the index.** This is exactly the kind of study the proposal says could misattribute drift if ANN were used. Here they used exact or embedded scoring.
- **ReproRAG**: "On The Reproducibility Limitations of RAG Systems" (arXiv 2509.18869). It studies uncertainty across embedding models, precision, retrieval algorithms, hardware and distributed execution, using Exact Match Rate, Jaccard and Kendall τ. A search snippet reports a mean top-50 Overlap Coefficient of about 0.80 "after the data insertion". The abstract does not frame this as relevance effectiveness. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.18869). Verdict: **partial overlap.** It touches result instability after insertions and across retrieval algorithms, but measures overlap and consistency, not labelled effectiveness, and has no snapshots or decomposition. **Worth a full-text read** for its insertion experiment.
- "Measuring Retrieval Freshness and Accuracy Degradation in Continuous ETL-Driven RAG Systems" (ResearchGate 401642877). The page returned 403, so the authors, venue and content are unverified. — [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/401642877_Measuring_Retrieval_Freshness_and_Accuracy_Degradation_in_Continuous_ETL-Driven_RAG_Systems). Verdict: **unknown and possibly close.** Read it before claiming novelty.
- **LiveVectorLake**: arXiv 2601.05270. A versioned knowledge-base architecture for streaming vector updates and temporal retrieval. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2601.05270). This comes from a snippet only. Verdict: **likely different** (a systems architecture).
- Grey literature: a Towards Data Science article claims HNSW recall@5 fell from 0.94 at 50k vectors to 0.83 at 200k at fixed ef_search, while Flat degraded less. — [TDS](https://towardsdatascience.com/hnsw-at-scale-why-your-rag-system-gets-worse-as-the-vector-database-grows/). See also [TianPan blog on RAG freshness](https://tianpan.co/blog/2026/04/10/rag-freshness-problem-stale-embeddings-silent-failure). Neither is peer reviewed. They show that practitioners recognise the phenomenon and do not give a rigorous study.
- A snippet from vendor docs: OceanBase does an incremental refresh above 10k new entries and a full refresh when new data exceeds 20% of the original. — [OceanBase docs](https://www.oceanbase.ai/docs/vector-index-monitor-and-maintain). These are industry heuristics with no effectiveness evaluation.

### Inferences
- The practitioner-level folk knowledge ("HNSW gets worse as the DB grows") weakens any claim that the *phenomenon* is unknown. The defensible novelty is the *measurement methodology*: an exact-vs-ANN decomposition on relevance-labelled longitudinal snapshots, comparing maintenance strategies.

### Gaps
- FreshQA, RealTimeQA, TemporalWiki and DynamicQA were not individually checked for index configuration, because the searches returned no index-maintenance angle for them.

## Q4. Does any benchmark report nDCG (not only k-NN recall) for ANN indexes under streaming updates?

### Takeaway
I found none. Streaming-ANN papers report k-NN recall against exact neighbours, mostly on synthetic insert/delete cycles over static datasets. The nearest item is a 2026 student presentation that uses MS MARCO embeddings but still measures recall against exact neighbours under synthetic query drift.

### Cited Findings
- **Nakacwa & Shaalan**, "Dynamic-Query Robustness of ANN Indexes under Time-Indexed Drift", Harrisburg University presentation (Other Works 28), 24 Apr 2026. HNSW and IVF-Flat with MS MARCO embeddings. Query drift is modelled as stochastic corruption on the unit hypersphere, with a budget set by compute or network delay. The metric is recall against "exact nearest-neighbour ground truth". The finding is that drift shifts the optimal settings toward shallower search. — [Digital Commons](https://digitalcommons.harrisburgu.edu/other-works/28/). Verdict: **partially overlaps on "ANN error under time-indexed drift", and differs in substance.** It has synthetic query perturbation rather than corpus evolution, no relevance labels, no incremental vs rebuild comparison, and no exact-vs-ANN decomposition of effectiveness change. It is not peer reviewed.
- **FreshDiskANN**: Singh et al., 2021 (arXiv 2105.09613). Recall over many cycles of streaming updates; the index "stabilizes at a lower recall value compared to static indexes." — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2105.09613). Verdict: **partial overlap on the index-decay mechanism.** k-NN recall only, synthetic churn, no relevance labels, no snapshots.
- **IP-DiskANN**: "In-Place Updates of a Graph Index for Streaming ANN Search" (arXiv 2502.13826, 2025). Stable recall over lengthy update patterns. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.13826). Verdict: **partial overlap** (the in-place maintenance strategy itself). k-NN recall only.
- **Aden-Ali, Ferhatosmanoglu, Greaves-Tunnell, Mishra, Wagner**, "Quantization for Vector Search under Streaming Updates", arXiv 2512.18335 (20 Dec 2025). Standard quantization loses effectiveness under inserts and deletes. They give dynamic quantization with guarantees and bounded disk I/O per update. — [arXiv abs](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.18335). Verdict: **partial overlap on "stale quantization".** The metric is k-NN recall. The fetch-tool summary named Deep1B/BigANN and a rebuild-vs-incremental comparison, but that summary was not verified against the text. There is no relevance-labelled evaluation.
- "Enhancing HNSW Index for Real-Time Updates: Addressing Unreachable Points and Performance Degradation" (arXiv 2407.07871). — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.07871). Verdict: **partial overlap on mechanism** (unreachable points after updates). k-NN recall.
- **Robustness-δ@K**: Wang, Zhang, Lu, Chen, Tan (arXiv 2507.00379, rev. Apr 2026). Argues that average recall hides per-query variability, and claims "more robust vector indexes yield better application performance". No temporal or update dimension. — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2507.00379). Verdict: **different, but supports the motivation.** It links ANN quality to downstream application metrics, statically.
- Static precedent for ANN-vs-exact *effectiveness* (MRR/nDCG) in dense retrieval: Tu et al., "Approximate Nearest Neighbor Search and Lightweight Dense Vector Reranking", ICTIR 2020. — [PDF](https://cs.uwaterloo.ca/~jimmylin/publications/Tu_etal_ICTIR2020.pdf). Verdict: **different** (static corpus). It shows that measuring the ANN-vs-exact effectiveness gap is established practice, just not longitudinally.

### Inferences
- The two halves of the proposal are each anticipated: (i) ANN index quality decays under streaming updates and stale quantizers (FreshDiskANN, IP-DiskANN, Aden-Ali et al.), and (ii) effectiveness drifts across temporal snapshots (LongEval, "Still Fresh?", StreamingQA work). **Joining them** has not been found in published work: relevance-labelled metrics, an exact-search counterfactual per snapshot, and rebuild vs incremental vs in-place maintenance compared. The novelty claim should be phrased narrowly as this combination plus the attribution methodology, not as "nobody knew ANN indexes degrade."

### Gaps
- Not checked: NeurIPS'23 Big-ANN streaming track and its follow-ups, which use k-NN recall to my knowledge (unverified here). VIBE and ann-benchmarks are static. LongEval participant papers using HNSW/FAISS were not systematically checked; one search found none discussing index error.
- Full texts of AURORA, ReproRAG, the ETL-RAG freshness paper, L²R and GradNormIR should be read to confirm they contain no flat-vs-ANN longitudinal ablation.
