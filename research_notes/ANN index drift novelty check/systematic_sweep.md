## Q1. Does any work already combine ANN indexes + temporal/evolving corpora + effectiveness measured with relevance labels (the core claim, and the exact-vs-index decomposition)?

### Takeaway
No hit found does the core combination: none measures ANN-index error separately from exact search across snapshots of a longitudinal test collection with relevance labels, or warns that index error could be mistaken for temporal drift. The closest prior work is PLAID SHIRTTT (SIGIR 2024). It measures nDCG on a time-ordered stream and shows that a stale index structure (centroids fit early in the stream) can collapse effectiveness. It compares against an "oracle" index built on the whole collection, not against exact search, and it does not frame this as a confound in longitudinal evaluation. A reviewer would cite it as strong partial overlap.

### Cited Findings
- **PLAID SHIRTTT for Large-Scale Streaming Dense Retrieval**, Lawrie, Kayi, Yang, Mayfield, Oard, SIGIR 2024 (short). arXiv 2405.00975, DOI 10.1145/3626772.3657964 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.00975). Documents are sorted by publication date (ClueWeb09, 504M docs; NeuCLIR, 10M docs) and indexed as a stream. When Chinese NeuCLIR centroids were fit on only the first 5% of documents, nDCG@20 fell from 0.440 to 0.047. The paper says "clustering once early in the stream does not work well". Its hierarchical re-sharding reaches about 96% of an Oracle single-centroid-set index (nDCG'@20 0.431 vs 0.448 on ClueWeb09) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2405.00975). **Verdict: partially overlaps (the strongest threat).** Overlap: index-structure staleness degrades labelled effectiveness over a time-ordered stream, and it compares rebuild-style vs early-fit index maintenance. Not covered: an exact/brute-force reference, separating index error from model or collection drift, a LongEval-style snapshot evaluation, graph indexes (HNSW), in-place vs incremental vs rebuild for graph indexes, and a label-free signal. Its baselines are BM25 and PSQ+HMM, and the paper does not define a "temporal persistence" measure.
- **Operational Advice for Dense and Sparse Retrievers: HNSW, Flat, or Inverted Indexes?**, Jimmy Lin, arXiv 2409.06464 (ACL 2025 Industry) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2409.06464), [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-industry.61.pdf). It compares HNSW with flat (exact) indexes on effectiveness, so it is an exact-vs-ANN nDCG comparison, but on static BEIR corpora. **Verdict: partially overlaps (decomposition on a static corpus only; no time).**
- **Anserini Gets Dense Retrieval: Integration of Lucene's HNSW Indexes**, arXiv 2304.12139 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2304.12139). Reports that HNSW scores are usually somewhat below exact search, on static collections. **Verdict: different (static).**
- A 2026 arXiv paper on hybrid product search reports HNSW costing about .003 nDCG@10 against exact search (static) — [arXiv 2611.11031](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.11031) (number from the search snippet; not fetched). **Verdict: different (static).**
- **Evaluation of Temporal Change in IR Test Collections**, Keller, Breuer, Schaer, ICTIR 2024, arXiv 2407.01373, DOI 10.1145/3664190.3672530 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.01373v1). Studies how far effectiveness evaluations generalize over time on TripClick, TREC-COVID and LongEval. It attributes change to the collection, topics and relevance perception, with no index-approximation component. **Verdict: different (it supplies the measurement framework the proposal would extend; no ANN component).**
- **Evaluating Temporal Persistence Using Replicability Measures**, Keller et al., arXiv 2308.10549 (LongEval CLEF 2023) — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2308.10549). **Verdict: different (no ANN).**
- **LongEval at CLEF 2025: Longitudinal Evaluation of IR Systems on Web and Scientific Data**, Cancellieri et al., arXiv 2509.17469 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2509.17469); repository record — [TU Wien repositum](https://repositum.tuwien.at/handle/20.500.12708/224590?mode=full). Measures nDCG and temporal-change measures for 19 teams. The record does not mention ANN or vector indexes. **Verdict: different.**
- **LongEval-Retrieval: French-English Dynamic Test Collection for Continuous Web Search Evaluation**, arXiv 2303.03229 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2303.03229v2). Collection paper. **Verdict: different.**
- LongEval participant papers using dense retrieval: AGH IR at LongEval 2025 (dense + cross-encoder; [CEUR Vol-4038 paper 276](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4038/paper_276.pdf)), DS@GT at LongEval 2025 (two-stage; [ResearchGate](https://www.researchgate.net/publication/393655650_DSGT_at_LongEval_Evaluating_Temporal_Performance_in_Web_Search_Systems_and_Topics_with_Two-Stage_Retrieval)), and "Temporal Stability in Scientific IR: A Three-System Analysis for CLEF LongEval 2026" (Kumaran D et al.; CEUR Vol-4283, e.g. [paper379](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4283/paper379.pdf) / [paper384](https://ceur-ws.org/Vol-4283/paper384.pdf); which number is which was not confirmed). None of the snippets or abstracts I saw report ANN-vs-exact analysis. **Verdict: different (based on abstracts and snippets; full texts not read).**
- **Submitted and Diagnostic Analysis of Full-Text Temporal Retrieval for LongEval-Sci**, Yingdong Yang, Haijian Wu, arXiv 2607.04088 (LongEval 2026) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.04088). Uses a Qwen3-Embedding-4B dense baseline served through an embedding service and makes no exact-vs-approximate distinction. **Verdict: different on the decomposition. Partial overlap on the label-free monitoring idea (see Q3).**
- **Exploring the Practicality of Generative Retrieval on Dynamic Corpora**, Yoon et al., EMNLP 2024, arXiv 2305.18952 — [ACL Anthology](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.755.pdf). Compares dual encoders and generative retrieval on growing corpora (StreamingQA-based) with labelled effectiveness. The concern there is the model and the cost of re-indexing, not ANN approximation error. **Verdict: different.**
- **StreamingQA**, Liška et al., arXiv 2205.11388 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2205.11388). A temporal QA benchmark. **Verdict: different (benchmark).**
- **MURR: Model Updating with Regularized Replay for Searching a Document Stream**, arXiv 2504.10250 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2504.10250). Updates the model on a document stream (authors not verified here). **Verdict: different (model updating, not index error).**

### Inferences
- The specific gap (ANN error as a confound in longitudinal evaluation, measured as nDCG under the index minus nDCG under exact search, per snapshot) still looks open. But PLAID SHIRTTT already shows empirically that index staleness can dominate effectiveness over a stream. The proposal should cite it and set itself apart: exact-search reference, graph indexes, LongEval snapshots, attribution framing.
- Static exact-vs-HNSW gaps are reported as small (about 0.003-0.02 nDCG@10). A reviewer will ask whether the temporal growth of that gap is ever large enough to matter. The proposal needs a measured effect size.

### Gaps
- Full texts of the LongEval 2025/2026 participant papers (CEUR Vol-4038 and Vol-4283) were not read. One of them may run FAISS/HNSW and note approximation effects in passing.
- Forward citations of Keller et al. 2024 and Lin 2024 were not listed (the Semantic Scholar citations endpoint was rate-limited).

## Q2. Rebuild vs incremental vs in-place maintenance: has this been compared with effectiveness (labels or task quality) rather than only recall@k?

### Takeaway
The ANN systems literature compares maintenance strategies at length (FreshDiskANN, SPFresh, MN-RU/Xiao et al., IP-DiskANN, CleANN, Wolverine, MERIT), almost always with recall@k against brute-force ground truth on SIFT/Deep-style vectors, not with relevance-labelled IR effectiveness. A few RAG and streaming papers compare rebuild vs incremental with nDCG or EM/F1, but they have no exact-search reference and no longitudinal test collection. **Verdict for the maintenance-comparison component: partially overlapped. Most novelty sits in measuring it with relevance labels on longitudinal snapshots, not in the comparison itself.**

### Cited Findings
- **Enhancing HNSW Index for Real-Time Updates: Addressing Unreachable Points and Performance Degradation**, Xiao et al., arXiv 2407.07871 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2407.07871). Repeated delete/reinsert on HNSW creates "unreachable points". On SIFT (5M), deleting and reinserting 5% for 3,000 rounds raises the unreachable fraction to 3-4% and lowers recall@1 by about 3% (figures from the search snippet). **Verdict: partially overlaps (the mechanism behind "index error grows as the corpus evolves", measured as recall on synthetic churn, with no labels or longitudinal IR).**
- **Wolverine: Highly Efficient Monotonic Search Path Repair for Graph-based ANN Index Updates**, PVLDB vol. 18 p. 2268 — [VLDB PDF](https://www.vldb.org/pvldb/vol18/p2268-zheng.pdf). **MERIT: Efficient In-Place Deletion for Dynamic Graph-Based ANN Indexes**, arXiv 2607.29173 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2607.29173). Both report recall@10, QPS and distance computations (per snippet). **Verdict: different (systems metrics only).**
- **In-Place Updates of a Graph Index for Streaming ANN Search (IP-DiskANN)**, arXiv 2502.13826 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.13826v1). **CleANN** (fully dynamic graph ANNS that keeps quality at static-build level) — [emergentmind entry](https://www.emergentmind.com/articles/2507.19802). **Verdict: different (recall-based; they are the "in-place" baselines the proposal should use).**
- **FreshDiskANN** (Singh et al. 2021, arXiv 2105.09613) and **SPFresh** (Xu et al., SOSP 2023): known baselines for incremental and in-place updates, evaluated on recall and latency. Not re-fetched in this sweep. **Verdict: different.**
- **From Static to Dynamic: A Streaming RAG Approach to Real-time Knowledge Base**, Yuzhou Zhu, arXiv 2508.05662 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2508.05662). Incremental index upserts over eight real-time streams. Reports Recall@10 (+3 points), +3.2 EM and +2.8 F1 on SQuAD. A web-search summary says it compares full rebuild with Faiss IVFPQ incremental upserts on Recall@K, MRR, nDCG@10 and EM/F1; the abstract does not confirm this. The abstract mentions no exact-search reference. **Verdict: partially overlaps (rebuild vs incremental with task quality; no exact baseline, no longitudinal labels, no attribution).**
- **SPI: Query-Depth-Adaptive Indexing for Streaming RAG in Vector Databases**, arXiv 2511.16681 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2511.16681). Streaming insertion without global rebuild. Per the snippet, rebuild baselines cost far more to update while search time stays about the same. **Verdict: different/partial (systems focus; effectiveness metric not verified).**
- **LiveVectorLake**, Tarun Prajapati, arXiv 2601.05270 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.05270). Versioned vector knowledge base. Reports re-processing of 10-15% vs 100% for a full re-index, plus latency. No relevance labels. **Verdict: different.**
- **Neuro-Symbolic Continual Indexing for Evolving RAG**, ACL Findings 2026 — [ACL Anthology PDF](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.495.pdf). The PDF could not be parsed, so content is not verified. **Verdict: unknown. Check manually.**
- **Drift-Adapter: Near Zero-Downtime Embedding Model Upgrades in Vector Databases**, Harshil Vejendla, EMNLP 2025, arXiv 2509.23471 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.23471). Compares an adapter with a full re-index after an embedding-model upgrade. Recovers 95-99% of re-embedding recall at more than 100x lower recompute cost (Recall@10, MRR). **Verdict: different (model-version drift, not corpus drift; reviewers may still cite it as "vector DB drift vs re-index").**
- **PANNS: recency-aware construction for graph ANNS** (2025, DOI 10.1145/3710848.3710867), **ANN Search over Temporal Vector Data** (TKDE 2026, DOI 10.1109/TKDE.2026.3722749), **Timestamp ANN Search** (ICDE 2025, DOI 10.1109/ICDE65448.2025.00228), **Versioned Unified Graph Index for Timestamp-Aware NN Search** (arXiv 2608.27663, [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.27663)), **BANCO: Drift-Aware Batched Bandits for Adaptive Proximity Graph Pruning** (WWW 2026, DOI 10.1145/3774904.3792396). Found through Semantic Scholar titles only. **Verdict: different (time as a filter or construction prior, or "drift" as an index-tuning signal). BANCO's "drift-aware" needs a check of its abstract; not verified.**
- Industry blog on tombstones degrading HNSW recall over time — [DEV Community](https://dev.to/kenwalger/vector-search-at-scale-why-your-index-isnt-as-healthy-as-you-think-1c19). **Verdict: different (non-academic, but shows practitioners know about it).**

## Q3. A label-free query-drift signal from the query-to-NN vs row-to-NN distance ratio: does it already exist?

### Takeaway
The underlying observation is published: out-of-distribution queries sit much farther from their nearest neighbours than in-distribution points or queries do. It underpins OOD-DiskANN and RoarGraph, and query-hardness measures (relative contrast, epsilon-hardness, Steiner-hardness) are label-free distance statistics. **Verdict: the signal itself is partially subsumed.** Novelty can rest only on using it as a longitudinal drift and maintenance trigger tied to labelled effectiveness change. One LongEval 2026 paper already uses label-free maintenance triggers, though they are collection-metadata signals, not embedding-distance ones.

### Cited Findings
- **OOD-DiskANN: Efficient and Scalable Graph ANNS for Out-of-Distribution Queries**, Jaiswal et al., arXiv 2211.12850 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/html/2211.12850v2). Shows that OOD query-to-NN distances are much larger than in-distribution ones, and that the k-NN of an OOD query are spread far apart. **Verdict: partially overlaps / largely subsumes the diagnostic statistic (not used over time, not linked to nDCG).**
- **RoarGraph: A Projected Bipartite Graph for Efficient Cross-Modal ANN Search**, VLDB 2024, arXiv 2408.08933 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2408.08933). Characterises OOD queries by their distance to nearest neighbours relative to base-to-base distances. **Verdict: partially overlaps (same statistic, cross-modal and static).**
- **Steiner-Hardness: A Query Hardness Measure for Graph-Based ANN Indexes**, Wang et al., PVLDB 17 p. 4668 — [VLDB PDF](https://www.vldb.org/pvldb/vol17/p4668-wang.pdf). Also covers relative contrast (mean distance over k-th NN distance) and epsilon-hardness (per snippet). **Verdict: partially overlaps (label-free per-query hardness; static).**
- **Evaluating and Generating Query Workloads for High Dimensional Vector Similarity Search**, Ceccarello, Levchenko, Ileana, Palpanas, arXiv 2606.14511 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.14511). Compares hardness measures and generates workloads that hit target hardness. No temporal angle. **Verdict: different/partial.**
- **VIBE: Vector Index Benchmark for Embeddings**, arXiv 2505.17810 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2505.17810). Includes OOD query settings (static). **Verdict: different.**
- **Coverage Is Not Redundancy: Maintenance Cost and Exposure of Query-Aware Admission Indexes in Vector Databases**, arXiv 2608.16043 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.16043). Maintains a query-aware sentinel index online under real COVID-19 workload drift in pgvector (0.33% ingest overhead). Measures hub exposure, not relevance. **Verdict: partially overlaps (query-workload drift in vector DBs with online maintenance; no labels; different signal).**
- **Yang & Wu, LongEval-Sci 2026** (arXiv 2607.04088) — [arXiv HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2607.04088). A weekly label-free update-monitoring policy with triggers on ingestion velocity and stale-coverage drift (staleness_rate, coverage_gap), framed as "a maintenance diagnostic rather than a ranking improvement claim". It does not involve the dense or ANN index. **Verdict: partially overlaps (label-free maintenance trigger inside LongEval, so a reviewer could cite it as prior art for the "signal" framing; it differs in using metadata, not embedding geometry).**
- **Robust Neural IR: An Adversarial and Out-of-distribution Perspective**, arXiv 2407.06992 — [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2407.06992). A survey of OOD in neural IR, model-side. **Verdict: different.**

### Inferences
- The proposal should present the distance ratio as an adaptation of established OOD/hardness statistics (citing OOD-DiskANN, RoarGraph, relative contrast) and claim novelty only for validating it as a predictor of longitudinal index-induced effectiveness loss.
- Theses and dissertations 2022-2026: no dissertation on dynamic vector search combined with temporal IR effectiveness turned up. The search surfaced only papers (IP-DiskANN, DIGRA, CleANN, the versioned graph index). Coverage of theses is weak (no ProQuest/EThOS/OATD access).
- Workshops: LongEval CLEF overviews and participants were checked (no ANN-error analysis seen). TempWeb, ReNeuIR and the ECIR reproducibility track were not specifically searched, because the database APIs failed. These are open gaps.
